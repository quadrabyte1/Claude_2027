## BEFORE YOU READ ANYTHING ELSE — THE GATING QUESTION

**Does Hartford's local Historic Preservation Ordinance (HPO) apply to 183 Kenyon Street?**

**Yes — definitively.** The prior footprint research (`183_kenyon_st_online_footprint.md`) confirmed this from the primary source: the Hartford HPO Covered Properties List (rev. Oct. 2018) lists Kenyon Street 41–193 (odd numbers) under Zone Z3, West End North Historic District. 183 is an odd number in that range. This is not ambiguous.

What this means: **all exterior work visible from a public street that requires a building permit must be approved by Hartford's Historic Properties Commission BEFORE a building permit will be issued.** This is a separate, parallel track from SHPO (the state tax-credit review), and neither substitutes for the other.

The critical nuance — spelled out below for each project — is that the HPO review gates the building permit. You cannot get the permit first and deal with historic review later. The sequence is: historic approval → building permit → work.

---

## HOW HARTFORD'S TWO-TRACK HISTORIC REVIEW WORKS

Hartford operates two commissions under its HPO:

| Commission | Properties It Covers | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **Historic Properties Commission (HPC)** | Properties in a *locally designated* historic district (like West End North / Z3) | Any exterior work visible from street that requires a building permit |
| **Historic Preservation Commission** | Properties individually listed on the CT or National Register but NOT in a local historic district | Same trigger |

183 Kenyon Street is in a locally designated district, so the **Historic Properties Commission** is your body. The two commissions share the same meeting schedule and staff contact but are technically distinct panels. When you call, specify: "I'm at 183 Kenyon Street, I'm in the West End North Z3 district — that's the Historic Properties Commission, correct?"

**Meeting schedule (verify currency):** Both Commissions meet every third Wednesday at 4:00 PM, Plaza Level Conference Room, 260 Constitution Plaza, Hartford.
- Source: [Hartford Preservation Alliance — HPC/HPC page](https://hartfordpreservation.org/historic-properties-preservation-commissions/)
- *Call to confirm 2026 meeting dates and submission deadlines — typically applications must be submitted 10–14 days before the meeting, but confirm this directly.*

**Statutory timeline:** Under CGS Chapter 97a, the Commission has 65 days from receipt of a complete application to hold a public hearing and issue a decision. Failure to act within 65 days constitutes approval by default. In practice, most routine approvals happen within one meeting cycle (4–6 weeks) if the application is complete on first submission.

**Fees (2018 schedule — verify for 2026):**
- Administrative review (minor work, staff-level): **$50**
- Public hearing (significant exterior work): **$200**
- Source: Hartford DDS fee schedule (eff. August 6, 2018). Call to confirm current fees before submitting.

## PROJECT 1 — FRONT PORCH REHABILITATION

### Why the front porch matters more than the back

The front porch is a primary character-defining feature of this Colonial Revival house. It is visible from Kenyon Street (a public street), it was specifically noted in the prior online footprint research (the Facebook listing describes it as an "open front porch"), and it was almost certainly documented in the NRHP nomination as part of the original 1916 design by Charles Octavius Whitmore. The HPO is unambiguous: this work gets reviewed.

---

### Checklist — Front Porch Rehabilitation

#### 1-A. Hartford Historic Properties Commission (HPC) — Certificate of Appropriateness / Historic Review Approval

- [ ] **Required: YES, without question.** The front porch is visible from a public street. Any work beyond cosmetic maintenance (painting, minor like-for-like board replacement) that requires a building permit also requires HPC approval first.
- [ ] **Application:** Complete Hartford's **Application for Historic Review** when initiating your building permit application. Submit to Hartford DDS / Development Services.
  - Application portal: [Apply for Historic Review — City of Hartford](https://www.hartfordct.gov/Government/Departments/DDS/DDS-Services/Apply-for-Historic-Review)
  - DDS main: [hartfordct.gov/DDS](https://www.hartfordct.gov/Government/Departments/DDS)
- [ ] **What to submit with your application:**
  - Existing-conditions photographs (all four elevations, plus close-ups of the porch structure, columns, railings, flooring, ceiling, steps)
  - Drawings or sketches showing proposed changes — scope description at minimum; detailed construction drawings if structural work is involved
  - Specifications for all materials to be used (species and dimension of wood, paint system, any replacement components)
  - For column/railing replacement: manufacturer cut sheets showing the proposed profile matches the original
  - *Note: The Hartford Preservation Alliance has a **Porch Tear Sheet** with specific best-practice guidance for porch work. Download it from [HPA Preservation Guidelines](https://hartfordpreservation.org/preservation-guidelines/) and give it to your contractor before you finalize scope.*
- [ ] **Standards applied:** The Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation (36 CFR Part 68). In practice this means: repair rather than replace wherever feasible; if replacement is necessary, match the original in material, profile, dimension, and character. No vinyl, no composite substitutes for wood columns or balustrades on a contributing Colonial Revival house without a compelling historic-evidence argument. The Commission uses these standards explicitly.
  - Reference: [Secretary of the Interior's Standards — NPS](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/historicpreservation/rehabilitation.htm)
- [ ] **Pre-application meeting: strongly recommended.** Before you finalize scope with a contractor, call Sandy Fry (Hartford DDS Senior Project Manager, 860-755-9222, sandy.fry@hartford.gov) or the Hartford Preservation Alliance (860-570-0331) for a pre-application conversation. Describe what you're planning. They will tell you whether it qualifies for administrative (staff-level, faster) or public-hearing review, and what documentation they'll want to see. This call can save a full meeting cycle.
- [ ] **Timeline:** Plan for 4–8 weeks from complete application submission to approval. If the Commission requests changes or conditions, add another cycle. Submit your HPO application BEFORE you schedule a contractor or order materials.

#### 1-B. CT SHPO (State Historic Preservation Office) — Only If Pursuing the §10-416 Tax Credit

- [ ] **If Thomas wants the CT Historic Homes Rehabilitation Tax Credit** (30% of QREs, max $30K credit): front porch rehabilitation is explicitly listed as eligible work in the HHRTC program. If front + back porch rehab combined clears $15,000 in qualifying construction costs (see `ct_historic_home_rebates_brief.md` Section 9A for what counts), the credit is in play.
- [ ] **SHPO Part 1 / Part 2 approval must be obtained BEFORE any work begins.** This is a hard rule. Costs incurred before Part 2 approval are not QREs regardless of how eligible the work itself would otherwise be.
  - Application portal: [ctofficeofthearts.smapply.org — HHRTC](https://ctofficeofthearts.smapply.org/prog/part_1_and_2_historic_homes_rehabilitation_tax_credit_applications/)
  - Program page: [portal.ct.gov — HHRTC](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/content/historic-preservation/02_review_funding_opportunities/tax-credits/historic-homes-rehabilitation-tax-credit)
  - CT SHPO contact: 860-500-2300 / [portal.ct.gov — SHPO](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/historic-preservation)
- [ ] **SHPO review is SEPARATE from and does not substitute for Hartford HPO review.** Both must happen. SHPO governs eligibility for the tax credit. HPO governs whether the permit is issued. You need both if you're doing the credit. If you're NOT pursuing the credit, SHPO review is not mandatory for the work itself — but following the Secretary of Interior's Standards is still required by HPO anyway.
- [ ] *Note from prior brief (Section 9E): The $15K QRE minimum must be in qualifying construction costs only. Architectural fees, permits, and the homeowner's own labor do not count. If you're bundling front + back porch, confirm with SHPO that they can be submitted under a single Part 2 application — call 860-500-2300 and ask explicitly.*

#### 1-C. Building Permit — Hartford Licenses & Inspections

- [ ] **When a permit IS required:**
  - Structural repairs or replacement: posts, beams, joists, ledger board, porch floor framing, foundation piers/footings
  - Rebuilding the porch deck surface (more than incidental board replacement)
  - New or replacement porch roof structure
  - Replacing or rebuilding steps with footings
  - Railing replacement affecting fall protection / egress safety
  - Any electrical work (porch lighting, outlets): **separate electrical permit, must be done by a CT E-1 or E-2 licensed electrician**
- [ ] **When a permit is generally NOT required (ordinary repairs / maintenance):**
  - Painting (including full repaint)
  - Replacing a few rotted deck boards in-kind (no structural change)
  - Replacing screen panels in existing frames
  - Cleaning, caulking, minor sealant work
  - Note: Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code (the current code) exempts "ordinary repairs" from permit requirements — but "ordinary repairs" explicitly excludes removal or cutting of structural supports, changes to egress, and electrical, plumbing, or mechanical work. When in doubt, call Licenses & Inspections at **(860) 757-9040** and describe your specific scope. Ask "does this require a permit?" and document who you spoke with and what they said.
  - Reference: [Connecticut State Building Code — Regulations, portal.ct.gov](https://portal.ct.gov/das/home/office-of-state-building-inspector/connecticut-state-building-code/regulations?language=en_US)
- [ ] **The contractor pulls the permit, in the contractor's name.** Not Thomas. If any contractor asks Thomas to pull the permit, that is a red flag and a liability trap — it shifts the liability to Thomas. This is not negotiable.
- [ ] **HPO approval must be in hand before the building permit application is processed.** Hartford's workflow routes historic properties through DDS review before the permit counter. Confirm this sequence with Licenses & Inspections when you call.

#### 1-D. EPA Lead-Safe (RRP) Certification

- [ ] **Required.** The house was built in 1916. Any contractor disturbing painted surfaces — sanding, scraping, replacing painted wood members — on a pre-1978 structure must be a **EPA RRP Lead-Safe certified firm** with a **certified renovator** on the job. This is federal law (40 CFR Part 745), not optional, not advisory.
- [ ] **Verify before hiring:** Search EPA's certified firm database: [cdxocsppapps.epa.gov/ocspp-oppt-lead/firm-search](https://cdxocsppapps.epa.gov/ocspp-oppt-lead/firm-search) — search by zip code 06105 and verify your specific contractor's firm is listed as currently certified.
- [ ] EPA Lead Hotline: **1-800-424-LEAD (5323)**

## PROJECT 2 — BACK PORCH REHABILITATION

### How the back porch differs from the front — and why it still needs review

The back porch is less likely to be the primary character-defining facade, but do not assume it is exempt from HPO review. The HPO trigger is **"visible from a public street and requires a building permit."** If the back porch can be seen from a side street, an alley, or a public right-of-way, it is potentially in scope. On a corner lot or a lot with side-street visibility, this matters. On Kenyon Street, an interior lot with a standard back yard, the visibility question turns on site geometry.

**Action before you assume anything:** Call Sandy Fry (860-755-9222) or Hartford DDS (860-757-9040) and ask: "My rear porch at 183 Kenyon Street — is it visible from any public street in a way that would trigger HPO review?" This is a 5-minute call that answers the question definitively.

---

### Checklist — Back Porch Rehabilitation

#### 2-A. Hartford Historic Properties Commission (HPC) — Historic Review

- [ ] **Likely required, but depends on visibility.** Apply the same analysis as the front porch. The Hartford HPO (§28-219 et seq., Municipal Code) covers all exterior work on covered properties that requires a permit and is visible from a public street. If the rear elevation faces any public ROW or alley, review is required.
- [ ] **Conservative approach (recommended by Hollis):** Treat it as required until you get a written or documented verbal confirmation from DDS staff that it is not. "I was told it wasn't" is not a defense when a stop-work order lands. Get it in writing or at minimum document who told you what and when.
- [ ] Same application process, standards, documentation, and fees as the front porch (see Section 1-A above).
- [ ] If the rear porch is not visible from any public street: **no HPO review required** for the rear porch as a stand-alone matter. The building permit requirement still applies based on scope (see 2-C below).

#### 2-B. CT SHPO — Only If Pursuing the §10-416 Tax Credit

- [ ] **Same analysis as front porch.** Back porch rehabilitation is eligible QRE work under HHRTC.
- [ ] **Bundling front + back porch under one Part 2 application is the right strategy** if pursuing the credit, since it maximizes the chance of clearing the $15,000 QRE floor. Confirm this aggregation is permissible with SHPO before structuring the project (call 860-500-2300).
- [ ] SHPO Part 1 / Part 2 approval must precede any work — same hard rule as front porch. No exceptions.
- [ ] If only the back porch is done and front is deferred: confirm whether the back porch alone reaches $15,000 in QREs before filing. If it doesn't, you're below the floor and get no credit. Bundling is the smarter move.

#### 2-C. Building Permit — Same Rules as Front Porch

- [ ] Structural work (framing, posts, footings, porch floor system, roof structure) = permit required.
- [ ] Painting, minor board replacement, caulking, screen replacement = typically no permit (ordinary repairs).
- [ ] Electrical (porch lighting, outlets) = separate electrical permit; E-1 or E-2 electrician required.
- [ ] Contractor pulls the permit. Thomas does not.
- [ ] Call (860) 757-9040 to confirm permit requirement for your specific scope.

#### 2-D. EPA Lead-Safe (RRP) Certification

- [ ] Same requirement as front porch. Mandatory on any work disturbing painted surfaces. Verify firm certification before hiring — same EPA database lookup.

## PROJECT 3 — BACK-YARD PATIO (NEW CONSTRUCTION, GROUND-LEVEL)

### The key facts about this project

A new, ground-level, at-grade patio — pavers, stone, or poured concrete — is fundamentally different in character from the porch rehabilitations. It is new construction (not rehabilitation of a historic feature), it is in the rear yard (typically not visible from the primary public street), and at-grade hardscape is treated differently under both building code and historic review frameworks.

---

### Checklist — Back-Yard Patio

#### 3-A. Hartford Historic Properties Commission (HPC) — Historic Review

- [ ] **Likely NOT required, with caveats.**
  - An at-grade patio in the rear yard is unlikely to trigger HPO review if it is (a) not visible from a public street and (b) does not require a building permit. Both conditions must hold.
  - The HPO trigger requires visibility from a public street AND a building permit. Remove either condition and HPO review generally does not apply.
  - **But:** If the patio design includes a pergola, overhead structure, or any attached structure, that may become permit-required and potentially HPO-visible depending on height and location. See 3-C below.
- [ ] **Confirm with DDS before proceeding.** Call Sandy Fry (860-755-9222) or DDS (860-757-9040): "I'm proposing a new at-grade paver patio in the rear yard at 183 Kenyon Street. Does this require a building permit, and if so, does it require HPO historic review?" Document the answer.
- [ ] **HPO on new site features / landscaping:** The Secretary of the Interior's Standards generally discourage new construction that is "distinguishable" from the historic property but do not prohibit it. An at-grade patio in the rear yard, not visible from the street, is unlikely to conflict with those standards. However, demolishing an existing historic site feature (a stone wall, a historic outbuilding, an original garden structure) to install the patio would be a different matter. Document what, if anything, exists in the back yard before starting.

#### 3-B. CT SHPO — §10-416 Tax Credit

- [ ] **Almost certainly does NOT qualify.** The HHRTC is a rehabilitation credit. New ground-level hardscape is new construction, not rehabilitation of a historic feature. Landscaping, driveways, sidewalks, and site improvements are explicitly excluded from QREs (see `ct_historic_home_rebates_brief.md` Section 9A). The patio cost should not be counted toward the $15,000 QRE floor for the credit — do not ask SHPO to include it.
- [ ] **No SHPO pre-approval required** for the patio as such. (If the patio is part of a larger project scope filed under Part 2, SHPO will note that the patio is not eligible work — but it will not block the credit for the eligible porch work.)

#### 3-C. Building Permit — Hartford Licenses & Inspections

- [ ] **Simple at-grade patio (pavers, stone, concrete, no roof, no footings, on-grade):**
  - Under the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, a simple on-grade patio with no structural framing, no roof, and no footings extending below grade is generally considered site work not requiring a building permit. The code exempts "ordinary repairs" and very low-level site features.
  - **However:** Hartford may additionally require a **zoning permit** (separate from a building permit) even for no-permit-required work, if the patio affects impervious surface coverage under the zoning code. Call the Planning & Zoning division at **(860) 757-9040** (ext. for Planning) or email oneplan@hartford.gov to confirm.
  - Reference: [Apply for Building and Trades Permit — Hartford](https://www.hartfordct.gov/Government/Departments/DDS/DDS-Services/Building-and-Trades-Permit)
- [ ] **If any of these apply, the calculus changes and a building permit will likely be required:**
  - Patio is attached to the house (ledger-attached)
  - Patio has a pergola, arbor, or roof structure
  - Patio involves concrete footings extending more than a few inches below grade
  - Patio is elevated more than 30" above adjacent grade at any point
  - Patio includes electrical (lighting, outlets)
  - Patio is a raised deck structure (wood framing) rather than at-grade hardscape
- [ ] **The contractor pulls any required permit.** Thomas does not.
- [ ] *Call to confirm:* **Hartford Licenses & Inspections, (860) 757-9040**, 8:30 AM–12:30 PM for walk-in applications.

#### 3-D. Zoning — Lot Coverage / Impervious Surface / Setbacks

- [ ] **This is the sleeper issue for the patio.** Even if no building permit is required, Hartford's zoning code limits how much of a lot can be covered by impervious surfaces (pavers, concrete, asphalt). A large patio on an existing developed lot can push the property over the allowable lot coverage percentage.
- [ ] **Hartford uses a form-based zoning code** (adopted post-2016). The specific dimensional standards — rear yard setback, side yard setback, maximum lot coverage percentage — for the West End Neighborhood district are not readily accessible via public web tools without interactive GIS use. **Call Hartford Planning & Zoning at (860) 757-9040 or email oneplan@hartford.gov**, give them the address (183 Kenyon Street, Hartford), and ask:
  1. What zoning district does this property fall in?
  2. What is the maximum lot coverage (impervious surface) percentage for that district?
  3. What is the rear yard setback for a ground-level accessory structure (patio)?
  4. Do I need a zoning permit for an at-grade patio that does not require a building permit?
- [ ] **Setback rule of thumb (verify — do not rely on this without confirming):** In most Hartford residential districts, rear yard setbacks for accessory structures are 5–10 feet from the property line, and side yard setbacks are 3–5 feet. But these vary by district. Confirm for the specific zone before siting the patio.
- [ ] **Source for Hartford zoning code:** [Hartford Zoning Regulations — Municode](https://library.municode.com/ct/hartford/codes/zoning_regulations)

#### 3-E. Call Before You Dig — 811 (Connecticut CBYD)

- [ ] **Required by Connecticut law.** Before any excavation — even shallow base preparation for pavers — Connecticut statute (CGS §16-345 et seq.) requires calling **811** (or the direct line, 1-800-922-4455) to have underground utilities marked. This is free and legally mandatory. You are personally liable for damage to unmarked utilities if you dig without calling.
- [ ] Website: [cbyd.com](https://www.cbyd.com)
- [ ] **Call at least 3 business days before excavation begins.** The utilities have 3 days to mark their lines.
- [ ] This applies even for shallow patio excavation (6–12 inches for base preparation). Do not skip this step.

#### 3-F. Tree Work (If Applicable)

- [ ] If the patio installation requires removing a tree: Connecticut requires a **licensed arborist** for tree removal (CT S-class arborist license, verified via [elicense.ct.gov](https://elicense.ct.gov)).
- [ ] Street trees and significant trees in Hartford may have additional city protections. Call Hartford's Parks & Recreation or Public Works before removing any tree near the street or of significant size.
- [ ] For any tree work adjacent to the historic structure, confirm it won't compromise historic site features noted in the NRHP nomination or SHPO file.

#### 3-G. EPA Lead-Safe (RRP) Certification — Patio Only

- [ ] An at-grade patio in the backyard typically does not disturb painted surfaces from the house, so EPA RRP certification is not triggered for the patio work itself.
- [ ] **Exception:** If the patio installation involves demolishing or modifying any painted structure (a painted retaining wall, a painted outbuilding, painted trim on the foundation), then RRP applies to that component. Confirm scope with your contractor.

## COMBINED RECOMMENDED SEQUENCE — ALL THREE PROJECTS

Do these in this order. Do not skip steps or run them out of sequence — the most common reason projects get stopped or credits get lost is jumping steps 3 or 5.

**Step 1 — Resolve the visibility question for the back porch (1 phone call)**
Call Sandy Fry at Hartford DDS (860-755-9222 / sandy.fry@hartford.gov) and ask: "Is the rear porch at 183 Kenyon Street visible from any public street in a way that triggers HPO review?" Document the answer. This call also establishes a staff relationship that will make every subsequent interaction faster.

**Step 2 — Decide whether you are pursuing the §10-416 CT Historic Homes Rehabilitation Tax Credit on the porches**
If yes: you must complete Steps 3 and 4 before any work starts. If no: Step 3 (SHPO) is optional, but Step 4 (HPO) is still mandatory.

**Step 3 — If pursuing the credit: file CT SHPO Part 1 / Part 2 BEFORE any work**
- Application: [ctofficeofthearts.smapply.org — HHRTC](https://ctofficeofthearts.smapply.org/prog/part_1_and_2_historic_homes_rehabilitation_tax_credit_applications/)
- Call SHPO first at 860-500-2300 to confirm that front + back porch can be submitted under a single Part 2 application (bundling QREs) and that the patio is excluded from the QRE calculation.
- Minimum 30-day SHPO review before any work begins. Do not schedule contractors until Part 1 approval is in hand.
- SHPO approval does not substitute for Hartford HPO review — you need both.

**Step 4 — Submit Hartford Historic Properties Commission Application for Historic Review**
- Complete the application via Hartford DDS before the building permit application is processed.
- Submit existing-condition photos, drawings/sketches of proposed work, material specifications.
- Request administrative (staff-level) review if the scope is sympathetic to the original character; anticipate public hearing review for significant alterations.
- Download and use the HPA Porch Tear Sheet before finalizing scope: [HPA Preservation Guidelines](https://hartfordpreservation.org/preservation-guidelines/)
- Timeline: 4–8 weeks from complete submission to approval. Plan accordingly.

**Step 5 — Finalize scope and drawings in coordination with HPO comments**
Do not finalize contractor scope until you know what the Commission will and won't approve. A contractor who has already ordered materials or framed up a quote based on vinyl columns will be unhappy when the Commission says no. Scope first, approval second, contractor third.

**Step 6 — Call Hartford Planning & Zoning about the patio (1 phone call)**
Phone: (860) 757-9040 / email: oneplan@hartford.gov. Get the zoning district, lot coverage limits, setback requirements, and whether a zoning permit is needed for the patio. This takes 15 minutes and prevents a surprise stop-work on the patio.

**Step 7 — Hire credential-vetted contractors**
Before hiring anyone, Hollis will run the credential screen: CT HIC registration (elicense.ct.gov), general liability + workers' comp COI, EPA RRP lead-safe certification, and SHPO project history for the porch work. Come back to Hollis at this stage for the shortlist. Do not hire before the screen.

**Step 8 — Contractor pulls building permit(s)**
The contractor pulls the building permit. Thomas does not. The building permit is where the liability lives. For electrical work (porch lighting, outlets), a separate electrical permit is required; the electrician must be CT E-1 or E-2 licensed.

**Step 9 — Call 811 before patio excavation**
At least 3 business days before any digging. 811 or cbyd.com. Free, required by law.

**Step 10 — Work proceeds; inspections as required**
For permitted work: the building department will specify required inspections (framing, footing, final). Contractor schedules these. Do not cover framed or structural work before the framing inspection.

**Step 11 — If pursuing credit: file SHPO Part 3 after porch work is complete**
Submit completion documentation (receipts, photos) to SHPO for the credit voucher. Do not skip this — no Part 3 filing means no credit voucher, regardless of how well the work was done.

## CONCERNS — READ BEFORE SPENDING A DOLLAR

**1. HPO review is not a suggestion — it is a hard gate on the building permit.** Hartford's DDS will not issue a building permit for covered properties without Historic Properties Commission approval. If a contractor says "let's get started and worry about permits later," that is not an option at this address. A stop-work order on a partially reconstructed porch is significantly more expensive than taking the 4–8 weeks to get approval before starting.

**2. SHPO pre-approval is equally non-negotiable if pursuing the tax credit.** Costs incurred before Part 2 approval are permanently disqualified from the QRE calculation. There is no exception, no retroactive approval, no workaround. File first. Build second. This was covered in the prior rebates brief (Section 9) and bears repeating here because this is the single rule that disqualifies the most applicants.

**3. Material substitutions will get flagged.** This is a 1916 Colonial Revival with an identified character-defining open front porch. Vinyl columns, composite railings that don't match the original profile, or composite decking on the front porch will face pushback from the Commission. Budget for wood (or fiber cement where historically appropriate) and for a professional carpenter or millwork shop capable of matching existing profiles. This is not where to cut corners.

**4. The back porch visibility question is not rhetorical.** If it turns out the back porch IS visible from a public right-of-way and Thomas proceeds without HPO review, the Commission has enforcement authority — including requiring restoration of unauthorized work at the owner's expense. Make the phone call (Step 1) before touching the back porch.

**5. Bundling both porches under one SHPO Part 2 application is smart — but confirm it is allowed.** If SHPO treats each porch as a separate application requiring its own $15,000 QRE floor, the math may not work individually. One 10-minute call to SHPO (860-500-2300) resolves this question before any commitments are made.

**6. The patio's impervious surface impact on the lot is unknown.** With a 9,583 sq ft lot and an existing house of 3,146 sq ft footprint, plus existing driveway/walkway coverage, the lot may already be near the zoning coverage limit. A large patio could push it over. This is a $0 zoning question that prevents a potentially expensive tear-out later. Make the call to Planning & Zoning (Step 6) before finalizing patio size and materials.

**7. EPA RRP certification is often misrepresented.** A contractor who says "I'm lead-safe trained" is not the same as a contractor whose **firm** is EPA RRP certified. The requirement is at the **firm** level, not just the individual renovator level. Verify at the EPA firm search database before signing any contract for the porch work.

**8. Fee schedule dates are old.** The Hartford DDS fee schedule confirmed is dated August 6, 2018. Fees may have been updated. Call DDS to confirm current application fees before submitting to avoid a rejected application.

**9. "HOA / deed restrictions"** — Kenyon Street does not appear to have an HOA. No covenant or deed restriction affecting exterior changes was identified in the prior footprint research. However, when Thomas pulls the current deed (advised in the footprint brief, Step 4 — SearchIQS), scan it for any recorded restrictions. Unlikely to be relevant but worth 5 minutes of review.
