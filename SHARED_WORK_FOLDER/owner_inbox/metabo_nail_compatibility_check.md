# Metabo HPT Nail Compatibility Check
**Prepared by:** Hollis — Home-Improvement Concierge / Owner's Advocate
**Date:** 2026-05-15
**Cart:** NR90ADS1M framing nailer · NH90ABM palm nailer · 15131M nails (2-3/8" × 0.113", 30°, paper-tape, clipped-head, hot-dipped galvanized)

---

## 1. Bottom Line

The nails are **correct for the framing nailer** — every spec matches. They are **not correct for the palm nailer as sold**: the 15131M nails are 2-3/8" long, and the NH90ABM's published minimum nail length is 2-1/2". You will need a separate box of loose framing nails at or above 2-1/2" for palm-nailer use. Do not check out expecting these collated strips to feed both tools.

## 3. Palm Nailer (NH90ABM) vs. Nails (15131M)

| Spec | NH90ABM Requires | 15131M Offers | Match? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feed type | Loose / bulk single nail | Paper-tape collated strip | NO (strip must be broken apart) |
| Nail length | **2-1/2" – 3-1/2"** | **2-3/8"** | **NO — 1/8" short of minimum** |
| Nail diameter | 0.113" – 0.162" | 0.113" | YES |
| Head style | Standard framing head (magnetic nose hold) | Clipped head | YES (clipped head feeds fine) |

**Two issues:**

1. **Collation:** Palm nailers do not accept loaded strips. You would need to break individual nails off the paper-tape strip before loading them one at a time into the magnetic nose. This is physically possible and a common field practice — paper-tape collation separates easily — but it is slow and the reason most contractors keep a separate box of loose bulk nails for palm-nailer work.

2. **Length (the harder problem):** The NH90ABM's published minimum nail length is **2-1/2"**. The 15131M nails are **2-3/8"** — 1/8" shorter than spec. Running nails below the minimum length risks short-driving, incomplete seating, increased piston wear, and potential double-fire or blank-fire. **Do not use 2-3/8" nails in this palm nailer.**

**What you need for the palm nailer:** A box of loose (bulk) common nails or joist-hanger nails, 2-1/2" or longer, 0.113"–0.162" diameter. Standard 10d common nails (3") or 16d sinkers (3-1/4") work well for joist hanger and structural connector applications, which is the primary use case for a palm nailer. Pick these up separately — they are inexpensive and widely stocked.

## 4. Concerns / Gotchas

**a) Palm nailer length mismatch — buy separate nails before you leave the store.**
The 2-3/8" nails in the cart fall below the NH90ABM's 2-1/2" minimum. This is the clearest stop-sign in this cart. If the palm nailer is going to see use (joist hangers, hurricane ties, tight-corner framing), grab a box of 10d or 16d bulk loose common nails in the fastener aisle before checkout.

**b) Ring shank — verify your application.**
The 15131M has a **ring shank**, not a smooth shank. Ring shank provides superior withdrawal resistance (good for decking, sheathing, subflooring) but is harder to pull if you need to adjust framing after driving. For standard wall framing that may need modification, a smooth-shank framing nail is more forgiving. If this is structural framing that won't move, ring shank is fine or even preferred.

**c) "15131M" is the Lowe's-exclusive SKU; "15131MHPT" is the standard retail SKU.**
The "M" suffix on Metabo HPT model numbers denotes a Lowe's-exclusive variant. The specifications are identical — same nail, same specs — but if you ever need to reorder elsewhere, search for 15131MHPT or equivalent.

**d) Hot-dipped galvanized = exterior/pressure-treated compatible.**
HDG is the correct coating for treated lumber (ACQ, CA) and exterior applications. If any of this framing involves treated lumber (deck framing, sill plates, PT beams), HDG is not just acceptable — it is required. Bright (uncoated) nails will corrode rapidly in contact with treated lumber. Good choice.

**e) Count check.**
One box = ~2,000 nails for the framing nailer. Estimate your nail count before buying a single box — large framing jobs can burn through 2,000 nails quickly. A second box may be prudent depending on scope.
