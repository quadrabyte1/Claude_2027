## REGISTRY-STATUS REASONING

**Step 1 — Is the West End North Historic District on the NRHP?** Yes. Listed July 25, 1985, NPS reference 85001618, documented in the NPS nomination form and confirmed by Wikipedia, WECA, Hartford Preservation Alliance, and the NPS NPGallery record.

**Step 2 — Is Kenyon Street within the district boundary?** Yes. The NRHP nomination boundary language states: *"Kenyon Street: #28 to #208 (not including #37), inclusive."* 183 is within this range. Kenyon Street is not peripheral to this district — it is one of the three founding streets, named for the original landowner who platted the subdivision.

**Step 3 — Is 183 specifically a contributing resource (not a non-contributing one)?** Yes. The NRHP nomination's continuation sheet individual property list explicitly marks 183 Kenyon Street as **"C" (contributing) — Colonial Revival house, 1916, Charles O. Whitmore.** The district has 291 contributing resources out of ~297 buildings; only 6 are non-contributing. 183 is not among the non-contributing ones.

**Step 4 — Is it also covered by Hartford's local HPO?** Yes. The Hartford HPO covered-properties list (rev. Oct. 2018) includes Kenyon Street 41–193 (odd numbers) in the West End North zone (Z3). 183 is odd and within that range. This means **Hartford's Historic Properties Commission must approve all visible exterior work requiring a building permit before any permit is issued.**

**Step 5 — Is it individually listed on the NRHP or CT State Register?** No evidence of individual listing. It is a contributing resource within a listed district — not a standalone listing. For CT HHRTC purposes, this is sufficient (the statute covers contributing resources within listed districts).

**Step 6 — Is it also on the CT State Register?** The CT State Register of Historic Places generally includes all NRHP-listed properties and districts automatically. The West End North Historic District, being on the NRHP since 1985, should be on the CT State Register as well. This was not independently verified via ConnCRIS in this session (ConnCRIS requires interactive map use), but the statutory presumption is strong. Thomas should confirm directly via ConnCRIS or a call to CT SHPO.

**Conclusion:** 183 Kenyon Street is a **confirmed contributing resource within an NRHP-listed historic district**, covered by Hartford's local HPO. It qualifies for CT HHRTC consideration. The NRHP coverage also means it is almost certainly on the CT State Register. Individual NRHP listing does not exist and is not needed for HHRTC eligibility.

## CONCERNS

**1. The address was given as "West Hartford" — it is Hartford.** This is not a minor clerical issue. The building department is different (Hartford DDS, not West Hartford Building Department). The Historic District Commission with authority over this property is **Hartford's Historic Properties Commission**, not West Hartford's. The assessor is Hartford's. Permits are pulled through Hartford. If Thomas has been dealing with West Hartford's building department, he has been dealing with the wrong office. Verify with Thomas.

**2. The local HPO adds a layer on top of the NRHP/SHPO layer.** For any visible exterior work needing a building permit, Thomas must get Hartford Historic Properties Commission approval *in addition to* CT SHPO pre-approval for the HHRTC. Two review tracks, not one. The HPO review is technically for local designation compliance; the SHPO Part 1/Part 2 process is for the tax credit. Mixing them up causes delays. SHPO pre-approval must come first; it does not substitute for Hartford HPO review.

**3. Year built discrepancy: 1915 vs. 1916.** Redfin and the Facebook listing say "c.1915." The NRHP nomination and the Hartford architect directory say 1916. The architect directory is derived from primary source research (CT Historical Bulletin) and the NRHP nomination was prepared by professional architectural historians. The 1916 date is more reliable. Assessor's records (once accessible) will give the official year-built used for permit and tax purposes.

**4. The architect directory was last revised September 2013.** The HPO covered-properties list was last revised October 2018. Neither is live. Use them as strong corroborating evidence, not as the definitive real-time record. Call CT SHPO and Hartford DDS to confirm current status before filing anything.

**5. ConnCRIS / CT State Register confirmation is still pending.** This research confirmed NRHP district status from the NPS nomination itself (primary source). What has not been independently confirmed via ConnCRIS is whether 183 is separately recorded in the CT State Register's GIS system as an individual resource or only appears as part of the district boundary. For most purposes this distinction does not matter — the district listing suffices for HHRTC — but if SHPO pulls the ConnCRIS record and it shows the property outside the district polygon due to a GIS mapping error, that would create a problem. Confirm via ConnCRIS before filing.

**6. The last sale was 1998 at $196,000.** No recent deed, permit, or sale record was found. The current owner of record was not confirmed through a public-facing source. Hartford land records (SearchIQS) were inaccessible during this session. Thomas should pull the current deed to confirm ownership before filing any HHRTC application in his name.

**7. Interior character-defining features are significant here.** The Facebook listing describes leaded glass windows, canvas ceilings, picture-frame moldings, original butler's pantry, sleeping porch, original claw-foot tub. These are period features that are almost certainly character-defining under the Secretary of the Interior's Standards. Any project that touches them — replacement windows, ceiling work, bathroom renovation — requires careful SHPO coordination. "Updating" leaded glass windows with vinyl replacements would likely disqualify the entire HHRTC application and potentially trigger HPO enforcement.
