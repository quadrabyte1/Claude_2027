## Scope

These instructions cover the complete construction of the 23'-6" × 16'-3" attached deck at the West Hartford property, as defined in the v17 plan and v17 Bill of Materials (BOM). The deck sits on an existing concrete patio at a maximum finished height of 12" above grade. Foundation is eight Pylex 50 screw piers (four per girder line) in two rows parallel to the house; framing is two doubled 2×10 PT girder lines running parallel to the house (x-direction) with face-hung 2×10 PT joists at 12" o.c. running perpendicular to the house (y-direction) in two segments each, all topping out at 11" AG; decking is MoistureShield Vision composite running parallel to the house in two halves of 11'-9" each, meeting at a center divider breaker board, with a 4-sided picture frame and MoistureShield composite fascia on three exposed sides. Two egress points: a left-edge single-step stair (42" wide, v12, unchanged) and a far-edge wide step (11'-9" long, v14, unchanged). All hardware is ACQ-compatible HDG or stainless throughout.

**Key geometry change from v15:** Girders now run parallel to the house (x-direction), not into the yard. Joists now run perpendicular to the house (y-direction), not parallel. Decking now runs parallel to the house, not perpendicular. This is the opposite of most framers' instinct on an attached deck. Read the "This Deck Specifically" cheat sheet in Appendix B before Day 1.

## How to use this document

Read the entire sequence before picking up a tool. The sections are in chronological build order — skipping ahead is how mistakes happen on a job this tight. Pay close attention to every **Don't Forget** callout marked 🟥. Those items exist because an experienced framer, working on instinct from previous jobs, would likely get them wrong on *this* deck specifically. The callouts are not generic safety reminders. They are the places where this design diverges from standard practice — and in v17 there are more of these than in any previous version because the entire framing orientation is reversed.

## Critical Pre-Build Checklist

Before any material is ordered, any lumber is delivered, or any work begins on-site, confirm each of the following in writing.

- [ ] **Permit pulled?** West Hartford building permit application submitted with v17 plan drawings (`deck_plan_v17.drawio` and printed pages), v17 BOM, Pylex 50 + Pylex 10555 load data sheets, DCA-6 Table 3A girder span check, Simpson LUS210Z and HU210-2 spec sheets, and Simpson DTT2Z spec sheet. Permit in hand before lumber delivery.
- [ ] **All BOM items confirmed in stock or on order?** Reference the v17 BOM. In particular: 72 × 12-ft MoistureShield Vision field planks (call Richards Building Supply — West Hartford — before ordering; if 12-ft is unavailable, shift to 36 × 20-ft); 55 × 8-ft 2×10 PT UC4B joists; 8 Pylex 50 piers + 8 Pylex 10555 saddles; 110 Simpson LUS210Z hangers; 2 Simpson HU210-2 hangers.
- [ ] **Frost line confirmed for West Hartford?** Connecticut frost line is **42"**. Pylex 50 shafts are 50" — helix tip must be a minimum 42" below patio surface. Confirm before pier purchase that the 50" shaft achieves this after accounting for any patio thickness.
- [ ] **Utility locates complete?** Call CT 811 (Call Before You Dig) **before punching any pier hole through the patio**, even though you are punching through concrete. Mandatory. There are no exceptions. Mark out date on permit timeline.
- [ ] **Patio condition assessed?** Walk the full 23'-6" × 16'-6" patio footprint before ordering. Note any existing cracks, settled sections, or hollow areas near the 8 proposed pier locations. A section that sounds hollow under foot pressure may not provide adequate lateral containment for the pier shaft during punching. Document any concerns and reassess pier placement if needed.
- [ ] **Colorway selected for MoistureShield Vision?** Cortex face-screw plugs are color-matched to the specific Vision product colorway. Do not order Cortex until colorway is final. Write down the Vision colorway name and the Cortex part number; both go in the homeowner file.
- [ ] **KDAT PT lumber on order?** Kiln-dried-after-treatment SYP may need 1–2 weeks lead time. Call Richards Building Supply or Curtis Lumber early.

## Section 0 — Pre-Construction

### Tools needed (full build)

| Category | Tools |
|----------|-------|
| Cutting | Circular saw (separate 40-tooth carbide blade for composite, 24-tooth for PT), miter/chop saw, reciprocating saw + bi-metal blades, jigsaw (PF notch cuts and step-opening joints) |
| Driving | Cordless drill-driver, impact driver, impact wrench (½" drive for carriage bolts and lag bolts) |
| Drilling | Hammer drill with ½" spade or auger bit for lag pilots; masonry bit + chisel set for patio punch finishing |
| Layout | 100-ft tape + two 25-ft tapes (keep a spare), chalk line + chalk (blue), marking spray paint (inverted, white or orange), framing square, speed square, mason's line + line levels |
| Leveling | 4-ft level, 6-ft level, post level (Pylex saddle plumb check), transit or laser level (essential — you are leveling across 23 ft in both axes) |
| Fastening | Torque wrench (½" drive, for carriage bolts at ledger clusters and pier saddle bolts), socket set + open-end wrenches, ratchet |
| Miscellaneous | Rubber mallet, sledgehammer (pier punching and driving), masonry chisel + 3-lb hammer (finishing punched pier holes), knee pads, sawhorses (2 pairs), angle grinder + diamond cup wheel (rental) |
| PPE | Safety glasses, hearing protection, cut-resistant gloves |

### Consumables — have on hand before Day 1

- Chalk (blue) + marking spray paint — layout and pier marks
- Blue tape + Sharpie — dimension references before framing is covered
- **End-grain sealant: Anchorseal 2 or Copper Green Wood Preservative, 2 qt minimum.** Keep the can at the saw station. Every PT cut end gets sealed the moment it's made. No exceptions.
- Construction adhesive — PL Premium or equivalent, for blocking glue-assist at butt joints
- Shop towels + bucket — adhesive cleanup
- 6-mil plastic sheeting + canvas tarp — protect staged PT lumber and MoistureShield bundles from rain overnight
- Drop cloths / construction plastic — protect patio surface during pier-punching work
- Drinking water and sunscreen

### Order timing

**PT lumber (KDAT SYP):** Call Richards Building Supply in Hartford metro first. KDAT SYP can take 1–2 weeks to arrive; standard wet-treated stock is usually on-hand but will shrink and cup post-install. Worth the wait.

**MoistureShield Vision boards:** Verify 12-ft stock availability locally before ordering 72 boards. If unavailable, 36 × 20-ft is the fallback (each yields two 11'-9" pieces with 6" waste). Do not order 16-ft — the v17 field plank cut length is 11'-9", which wastes 4'-3" per 16-ft board. Order early; decking is the hardest item to have arrive mid-build.

**Cortex face-screws + plugs:** Order only after Vision colorway is final. Color-matched plugs for the center divider breaker and all PF boards are permanently visible. Wrong colorway = permanently conspicuous plugs.

**Tiger Claw TC-G clips:** Confirm 700-clip availability at your decking supplier. This quantity may need to be pulled from back stock.

**Pylex piers + saddles:** The Pylex 10555 saddle (3.5" width) is specific to the doubled 2×10 fit. Confirm part number with the supplier — do not accept the wider saddle intended for tripled-ply girders.

### Inspection schedule

West Hartford typically requires four inspections on an attached deck permit:

1. **Footing/pier** — after all 8 piers are driven and grout collars are placed, before any framing bears on them. Inspector verifies pier tip depth (past 42" frost line), pier location within the patio footprint, and saddle elevation.
2. **Framing / pre-decking** — after all framing (ledger, girders, joists, blocking, hangers) is complete and before any decking or joist tape goes down. **This is the critical one.** The inspector needs to see every hanger, every nail pattern, the ledger connection clusters, and the DTT2Z lateral ties. Schedule this inspection before taping.
3. **Post-decking** — after decking (field planks, center divider breaker, PF boards), fascia, and both stair assemblies are complete. This deck is ≤ 12" AG so no guardrail is required by IRC R312, but confirm the IRC R312 interpretation with West Hartford building department at permit application.
4. **Final** — project complete; inspector walks it.

**Schedule the framing inspection BEFORE applying joist tape.** The inspector must see hanger nail patterns on bare wood. Once tape is down, that's over. Do not order decking delivery until the framing inspection is scheduled — inspector backlog can stretch 3–5 business days in West Hartford.

## Section 1 — Site Prep & Layout (Phase 1)

### What you're doing

Protecting the existing concrete patio from construction damage, establishing the precise deck footprint in the field, and confirming squareness before a single pier hole is punched. Errors here propagate through the entire build. Layout is free to fix now. After a pier is driven, it is not.

### Materials needed

- 6-mil plastic sheeting + canvas tarp (v17 BOM Section 10)
- Chalk line, marking spray paint, mason's line, line levels (v17 BOM Section 10)
- Tape measures × 2

### Tools needed

Two 25-ft tapes, 100-ft tape, chalk line, framing square, speed square, inverted spray paint, laser level or transit.

### Steps

1. Lay construction-grade 6-mil plastic across the entire patio surface. Overlap seams by 12". Tape all edges to the house foundation. Cover plastic with canvas tarp if rain is in the forecast or if heavy materials will be staged on it.
2. Mark the house-wall ledger line at the correct elevation: top of decking = 12.0" AG; subtract 1.0" (MoistureShield Vision thickness) and 9.25" (2×10 actual depth) = ledger top at **1.75" AG**. Snap a chalk line on the house wall at this elevation across the full 23'-0" ledger run.
3. Establish the framing origin at x=0', y=0' (house wall face at the left corner of the deck footprint). All measurements below are from this origin.
4. Mark all **8 pier center locations** on the patio surface using inverted spray paint. Put the chalk-line intersection at the center of each marked dot:
   - **Mid-girder line at y=8'-0" from house wall:** x = 0', 7'-8", 15'-4", 23'-0"
   - **Outer-rim girder line at y=16'-0" from house wall:** x = 0', 7'-8", 15'-4", 23'-0"
5. Run string lines to confirm both rows are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the house wall. Use a framing square at both ends; measure diagonals of the complete 8-pier rectangle (0', 0' to 23'-0", 16'-0"). Both diagonals must be equal within ¼". If they are not equal, reposition marks before punching anything.
6. Confirm all 8 pier marks fall within the 23'-6" × 16'-6" patio footprint. Measure from the patio edges, not from your chalk lines. If any mark is at or past the patio edge, stop and reassess.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **There are TWO girder lines with FOUR piers each — 8 piers total.** v15 had 6 piers (3 per girder line). v17 has 8 (4 per line). A framer familiar with the v15 plan will lay out 6 holes. Count the marks before punching.
- 🟥 **Pier x-spacing along each girder line is 7'-8", not 11'-6".** The v15 pier x-positions were x=0', 11'-6", 23'-0". v17 pier positions are x=0', 7'-8", 15'-4", 23'-0". These are completely different — check your string line with a tape before punching.
- 🟥 **Mid-girder row is at y=8'-0" exactly, not y=8'-1.5".** The v15 middle row was at y=8'-1.5". v17 is at y=8'-0". If you have any v15 marks on the patio, erase them before marking.
- 🟥 **The far edge of the deck (PF outer face) is at y=16'-3", not y=16'-6".** The patio extends to y=16'-6". A 3" strip of bare concrete will be visible along the far edge — this is intentional and documented. Do not try to stretch the framing to cover it.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

Before punching a single pier hole:
- [ ] All 8 pier marks are spray-painted on the patio surface, in two rows of 4
- [ ] Mid-girder row confirmed at y=8'-0" by tape measure from house wall
- [ ] Outer-rim girder row confirmed at y=16'-0" by tape measure from house wall
- [ ] x-positions along each row confirmed at 0', 7'-8", 15'-4", 23'-0" by tape measure
- [ ] Both rows confirmed parallel to each other; both perpendicular to house wall (framing square check at each end)
- [ ] Diagonal-equal check: both diagonals of the 8-pier rectangle are equal within ¼"
- [ ] All 8 pier marks confirmed inside patio footprint by tape measure from patio edges
- [ ] Plastic + tarp protection fully in place; no bare patio exposed under staging area
- [ ] CT 811 utility locate is complete and marks are on-site

## Section 2 — Ledger Installation (Phase 2)

### What you're doing

Installing the 2×10 PT ledger on the house wall and flashing it correctly. The ledger is the only connection between this deck and the house. Flashing failure here means rot that may not show for years. Do it right the first time.

### Materials needed

- 2×10 PT UC4B ledger board, 23'-0" (v17 BOM Section 2a)
- Henry Blueskin VP160 or Carlisle CCW-705 peel-and-stick flashing, 6" wide (v17 BOM Section 6)
- Z-flashing, aluminum, 2"×2" leg (v17 BOM Section 6)
- Kick-out flashing × 2 (v17 BOM Section 6)
- Simpson HU210-2 hangers × 2 (v17 BOM Section 4)
- ½" × 6" HDG carriage bolts + nuts + washers, 12 total (v17 BOM Section 5a)
- ½" × 4" HDG lag bolts, 34 total (v17 BOM Section 5a)
- 2×10 PT scrap backup blocks × 2 (v17 BOM Section 5a)
- Simpson DTT2Z lateral ties × 4 (v17 BOM Section 7)
- Ledger spacers / standoffs if siding extends behind ledger location (v17 BOM Section 6)
- End-grain sealant

### Tools needed

Hammer drill + ½" spade or auger bit, circular saw, impact wrench, torque wrench, 6-ft level, chalk line, tape measure, helper (basement access needed for back-blocking).

### Steps

1. Carefully cut the siding along the ledger run. Slot the siding at the top of the cut so that the top leg of the Z-flashing can slide behind it. Keep the cut tight.
2. Apply peel-and-stick flashing (Henry Blueskin VP160 or Carlisle CCW-705, 6" wide) to the sheathing/housewrap behind the ledger location. Lap it up the wall at least 4" above the top of where the ledger will sit; lap it down and over the bottom of the cut sill. Work from bottom to top so each strip overlaps the one below.
3. Set the 2×10 PT ledger at the marked elevation (ledger top = 1.75" AG) and tack it temporarily. Confirm top elevation with level across the full 23'-0" run. Adjust until level is within 1/8" across the entire run before making any permanent fastening.
4. Fasten the ledger using the v17 spec:
   - **At each of the two mid-girder-to-ledger connection points (x=0' and x=23'-0'):** install the Simpson HU210-2 hanger first (face-nail per Simpson spec sheet with specified fasteners — SDS ¼"×1½" or N10 as spec sheet requires; do NOT use 16d commons), then drive six ½" × 6" HDG carriage bolts per cluster. A helper must be inside the basement holding the backup block (2×10 PT scrap) against the interior face of the band joist while you drive bolts through. Do not do this solo.
   - **Between the two hanger clusters and along the full 23'-0" run:** ½" × 4" HDG lag bolts at 18" o.c., two-row staggered per IRC R507.9. Pre-drill every lag hole with the ½" spade or auger bit.
5. Set the Z-flashing cap over the ledger top. Slide the top leg up behind the siding (the cut from Step 1). **The top leg goes UNDER siding, not over it.** Do not caulk the top leg or the siding/Z-flashing interface.
6. Install kick-out flashing at both ends of the ledger where siding terminates.
7. Install Simpson DTT2Z lateral ties per IRC R507.9. Space 4 devices evenly along the ledger, staying clear of the HU210-2 hanger cluster zones. Confirm exact placement with the West Hartford building inspector before or at the framing inspection.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **Flashing before ledger, not after.** Peel-and-stick goes on the sheathing first, then the ledger goes up. If you mount the ledger and try to stuff flashing behind it, you will have gaps that will leak.
- 🟥 **v17 has TWO HU210-2 hanger clusters, not three.** v15 had three HU210-3 clusters (x=0', 11'-6", 23'-0') for three girders meeting the ledger. v17 mid-girder meets the ledger only at its two ends (x=0' and x=23'-0'). The outer-rim girder at y=16'-0" does NOT connect to the ledger at all. Do not install a third hanger cluster at x=11'-6".
- 🟥 **Use Simpson-specified fasteners for the HU210-2.** Read the spec sheet before opening a fastener box. Wrong fasteners mean the hanger doesn't achieve rated load and will fail inspection.
- 🟥 **Through-bolts at the HU210-2 cluster locations need a helper in the basement.** Back-blocking must contact the interior face of the band joist before the bolt goes through. Do not skip back-blocking.
- 🟥 **No caulk at the Z-flashing top leg / siding interface.** Caulk at this joint fails within 3–5 years in CT freeze-thaw cycles and traps water. Leave it free to drain.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] Peel-and-stick flashing applied to sheathing; top lap confirmed under siding cut
- [ ] Z-flashing top leg slides freely behind siding; no caulk at interface
- [ ] Kick-out flashing at both ends of ledger
- [ ] Ledger top elevation confirmed level across full 23'-0" run — within 1/8" end to end
- [ ] Two HU210-2 hangers installed at x=0' and x=23'-0' with correct fasteners per Simpson spec sheet
- [ ] Through-bolt clusters installed (6 per cluster) with back-blocking in place
- [ ] IRC lag pattern installed between clusters per R507.9
- [ ] DTT2Z lateral ties installed (4 devices)

### Inspection / sign-off note

West Hartford building department does not typically do a separate ledger inspection, but the framing inspector will verify the ledger connection detail at the pre-decking inspection. Have the HU210-2 spec sheet on-site.

## Section 3 — Pier Installation (Phase 3)

### What you're doing

Punching eight holes through the concrete patio and driving Pylex 50 screw piers below the 42" frost line. Each pier receives a Pylex 10555 saddle bracket set to the correct elevation to carry the doubled girders. This section is inspected before any framing bears on the piers.

### Materials needed

- Pylex 50 helical/screw piers × 8 (v17 BOM Section 1)
- Pylex 10555 saddle brackets × 8 (v17 BOM Section 1)
- ¼" cedar or HDPE shims × 16 (2 per saddle × 8 saddles) (v17 BOM Section 1)
- ½" × 3½" HDG carriage bolts + nuts + washers × 16 (saddle-to-shaft) (v17 BOM Section 1)
- Hydraulic cement or non-shrink grout, 10-lb bag × 2 (v17 BOM Section 1)
- Quikrete Vinyl Concrete Patcher, 10-lb tub × 1 (v17 BOM Section 1)

### Tools needed

Sledgehammer, angle grinder + diamond cup wheel (rental), cold chisel + 3-lb hammer, pipe wrench or breaker bar (Pylex driving), post level, transit or laser level, torque wrench.

### Steps

1. Set up the angle grinder with a diamond cup wheel. Score a 4" diameter circle around each pier mark to prevent slab cracking when you sledgehammer the center. Do all 8 marks before swinging the sledge.
2. Use the cold chisel + 3-lb hammer to open the slab at each mark; follow with the sledgehammer to knock through. Keep holes tight — a loose or oversize hole makes grout-collar work harder and reduces lateral shaft containment.
3. Drive each Pylex 50 pier at the punched hole location using the prescribed Pylex installation method (rotation with a pipe wrench or breaker bar). Drive until the helix tip clears 42" below the top of the patio slab. The Pylex 50 shaft is 50" — after driving to frost depth you should have approximately 7–8" of shaft above the patio surface.
4. Install the Pylex 10555 saddle bracket on each pier shaft top. Bolt with ½" × 3½" HDG carriage bolts per Pylex spec (2 per saddle). The saddle top must be set at the correct elevation: **1.75" AG** (top of decking at 12" minus 1.0" decking minus 9.25" joist depth). Use a transit or laser level to verify all 8 saddle tops are at the same elevation before tightening down. Shim with cedar or HDPE shims at the patio surface level where the slab surface is slightly uneven.
5. Install two ¼" cedar or HDPE shims inside each Pylex 10555 saddle — one shim per side — before the girder is lowered in. The doubled 2×10 (3.0" actual width) fits inside the 3.5" interior saddle with ¼" clearance per side. Shims must be rot-resistant (cedar or HDPE only — no OSB, no plywood, no composite of unknown composition). A dab of construction adhesive on the shim face before placement will prevent shims from walking out during saddle bolt tightening.
6. Plumb-check each pier shaft with a post level before final tightening. Adjust while you still can.
7. Pack hydraulic cement around each pier shaft at the patio surface (the grout collar). Keep the collar flush with or slightly below the patio surface. Let cure per product directions before loading.
8. After curing, touch up the surface cosmetically with Quikrete Vinyl Concrete Patcher around each collar.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **All 8 piers must land within the patio footprint.** Measure from the patio edge to each punched hole before driving. If a hole drifts outboard even a few inches, the pier tip is in native soil, not anchored in concrete at the surface. Patch and re-punch in the correct location.
- 🟥 **Saddle elevation is more important than saddle level in isolation.** All 8 saddle tops must be at the same elevation (± ¼" with shim allowance built in). Check with the transit or laser after each installation, not just visually. A saddle that is ¼" high shifts an entire girder line and throws the entire framing plane off.
- 🟥 **Do not hammer stalled piers.** If a Pylex pier stalls mid-drive — stops rotating under normal tool pressure — stop. The Pylex saddle-weld failure warning on the Pylex installation sheet applies here: driving with a sledgehammer on a stalled pier can shear the saddle weld. Call Pylex technical support or reassess. Do not pound through resistance.
- 🟥 **Call for pier inspection before any framing sits on the piers.** Inspector must verify pier depth and location. Girders placed on uninspected piers will require removal for inspection.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] All 8 piers driven; helix tips confirmed below 42" from patio surface (measure shaft above slab and subtract from 50" total shaft)
- [ ] All 8 saddle tops at 1.75" AG per laser level check — all within ± ¼" elevation (shim tolerance) of planned bearing height
- [ ] Saddle shims (cedar or HDPE, ¼" per side) seated in all 8 saddles
- [ ] Grout collars placed and cured at all 8 pier shaft penetrations
- [ ] **Pier / footing inspection passed and signed off before any framing bears on piers**

### Inspection / sign-off note

The pier / footing inspection is the first mandatory West Hartford building inspection. Call at least 2–3 business days in advance. Inspector typically verifies: pier tip depth past the 42" frost line, pier locations within the patio footprint, saddle elevations. Have the Pylex 50 spec sheet and Pylex 10555 load data sheet on-site.

## Section 4 — Girder Assembly & Installation (Phase 4)

### What you're doing

Building two doubled 2×10 PT girder lines running parallel to the house (x-direction) and setting them on the Pylex saddle brackets. Each girder line spans 23'-0" in two plies, with butt joints staggered over different piers. This is the key structural change in v17: girders run x-direction (parallel to house), not y-direction. Reference `deck_plan_v17.drawio` framing plan for girder layout confirmation.

### Materials needed

- 2×10 PT UC4B 16-ft girder plies × 4 (2 girder lines × 2 plies per line) (v17 BOM Section 2b)
- 2×10 PT UC4B 8-ft girder plies × 4 (2 girder lines × 2 plies per line) (v17 BOM Section 2b)
- GRK RSS or Simpson SDS ¼"×3" structural screws × ~96 for ply-to-ply, OR ½" × 3½" HDG carriage bolts × ~48 (v17 BOM Section 5b)
- ½" × 3" HDG lag bolts × 16 (girder-to-saddle, 2 per saddle × 8 saddles) (v17 BOM Section 1)
- Simpson HU210-2 hangers × 2 (already installed in ledger at x=0' and x=23'-0') (v17 BOM Section 4)
- End-grain sealant

### Tools needed

Sawhorses, circular saw, drill-driver, impact driver, impact wrench, torque wrench, 6-ft level, transit or laser level, tape measure.

### Steps

1. Assemble the mid-girder line first. On sawhorses, lay out the two plies for the mid-girder (bearing at y=8'-0"):
   - **Ply 1:** 16-ft piece from x=0' running to x=15'-4" (butt joint over the pier at x=15'-4"), then the 8-ft piece from x=15'-4" to x=23'-0".
   - **Ply 2:** 8-ft piece from x=0' to x=7'-8" (butt joint over the pier at x=7'-8"), then the 16-ft piece from x=7'-8" to x=23'-0".
   - This stagger ensures the two ply joints land on different piers. Neither ply is ever unsupported at the same cross-section as the other.
2. Stack both plies crown-up (crown = slight natural bow, convex side up). All crowns must go the same direction. Face-fasten with GRK RSS or SDS ¼"×3" structural screws staggered top/bottom/middle at 16" o.c. down the run, and at each butt joint (minimum 4 screws per side at the joint). Alternate: through-bolt with ½" × 3½" HDG carriage bolts at 24" o.c. per AWC 3-ply spec (applied to 2-ply: carriage bolts at 24" o.c. per engineer of record or per IRC).
3. Repeat Step 2 for the outer-rim girder line (bearing at y=16'-0"). Same splice rule: Ply 1 joint at x=15'-4"; Ply 2 joint at x=7'-8".
4. Set the mid-girder line. Seat the far ends (x=0' and x=23'-0') into the Simpson HU210-2 hangers on the ledger face. With a helper, lower the girder onto all four Pylex 10555 saddles at y=8'-0". Confirm shims are in place in each saddle before the girder lands. Do not fully tighten the saddle lag bolts until level is confirmed.
5. Set the outer-rim girder line. Lower it onto all four Pylex 10555 saddles at y=16'-0". The outer-rim girder does not connect to the ledger — it is free-spanning between its four pier saddles at y=16'-0". Do not run it back to the house.
6. With both girder lines set, check level along each girder run AND across the y-direction from mid-girder to outer-rim girder. Use the transit or laser — eye-balling is not adequate across 23 ft or 8 ft. Shim at saddle brackets as needed to achieve level across all 8 saddle tops and co-planar girder tops.
7. Drive ½" × 3" HDG lag bolts up through the saddle bottom into each girder bottom face (2 per saddle × 8 saddles = 16 lag bolts). Then tighten saddle carriage bolts to snug-plus-quarter-turn. Seat ledger-end HU210-2 hanger fasteners per Simpson spec.
8. Seal all cut ends immediately after cutting.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **Girders run PARALLEL to the house (x-direction), not into the yard.** Every experienced deck framer's instinct is for the main beams to run perpendicular to the house, into the yard. On this deck it is reversed. Before you set a single girder, reread this instruction and confirm it with the crew foreman.
- 🟥 **Ply joints must be on different piers — mandatory.** Ply 1 joints at x=15'-4". Ply 2 joints at x=7'-8". Never both plies at the same pier. Never midspan. If a framer proposes to splice both plies at the same pier to use shorter lumber drops, direct them back to this spec and to DCA-6.
- 🟥 **The outer-rim girder does NOT connect to the ledger.** It terminates at y=16'-0" bearing on four piers, period. No hanger at the ledger, no connection to the house. Do not add one.
- 🟥 **Doubled 2×10 girder is NOT tripled.** v15 used tripled 2×10 (3 × 1.5" = 4.5" wide). v17 uses doubled (3.0" wide). The Pylex 10555 saddle requires the 3.0" width to seat properly with ¼" shims per side. If any surplus tripled-ply boards from v15 work are on-site, do not use them.
- 🟥 **All 8 saddle tops must be co-planar and at 1.75" AG (bottom of girder).** This is the single most important elevation in the build. Every joist, every piece of decking, the 12" AG ceiling — all derive from here.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] Both girder lines assembled as doubled 2×10 with ply joints staggered: Ply 1 joints at x=15'-4", Ply 2 joints at x=7'-8"
- [ ] Girder tops level along each line — within 1/8" variation over 23'-0"
- [ ] Girder tops co-planar across the y-direction: mid-girder top to outer-rim girder top within 1/8"
- [ ] Perpendicular distance from ledger face to mid-girder house-side face = **8'-0" exactly**
- [ ] Perpendicular distance from mid-girder far face to outer-rim girder house-side face = **8'-0" exactly**
- [ ] All 8 saddle lag bolts driven; saddle carriage bolts torqued; HU210-2 hanger fasteners fully set
- [ ] All cut ends sealed

## Section 5 — Joist Installation (Phase 5)

### What you're doing

Hanging 50 joist segments (25 rows × 2 segments each) face-hung at 12" o.c., running y-direction (perpendicular to the house). Joists are split at the mid-girder — each row has an inboard segment (ledger face to mid-girder) and an outboard segment (mid-girder far face to outer-rim girder inboard face). The 25th row is the center joist at x=11'-6" framing, which provides the bearing point for the center divider breaker board. Reference `deck_plan_v17.drawio` for joist layout.

### Materials needed

- 2×10 PT UC4B 8-ft joists × 55 (50 segments needed, ~10% waste) (v17 BOM Section 2c)
- Simpson LUS210Z HDG joist hangers × 110 (100 needed + 10 overage) (v17 BOM Section 4)
- Simpson N10 (10d×1½") nails or SDS ¼"×1½" screws × 1,500 (v17 BOM Section 5c)
- Vycor Deck Protector or Trex Protect 2" tape × 11 rolls (515 lf total) (v17 BOM Section 6)
- End-grain sealant

### Tools needed

Circular saw, tape measure, speed square, drill-driver, impact driver, chalk line.

### Steps

1. Mark the joist layout on the top faces of the ledger, mid-girder (both faces), and outer-rim girder (inboard face only). Starting at x=0' (left end), mark at 12" o.c.: x = 0', 1'-0", 2'-0", ... 23'-0" for the 24 regular rows plus the off-grid center joist at x=11'-6". Total: 25 rows.
2. Install Simpson LUS210Z hangers on the ledger face at all 25 layout marks. Top of hanger flush with top of ledger.
3. Install Simpson LUS210Z hangers on the mid-girder house-side face at all 25 layout marks. Install Simpson LUS210Z hangers on the mid-girder far face at all 25 layout marks. Install Simpson LUS210Z hangers on the outer-rim girder inboard face at all 25 layout marks. Total LUS210Z installed: 100 hangers.
4. Cut joist segments from 8-ft stock to the clear span. Measure each bay independently after girders are set — do not assume all bays are identical. Clear span inboard: ledger face to mid-girder house-side face ≈ 7'-11¼". Clear span outboard: mid-girder far face to outer-rim girder inboard face ≈ 7'-11¼". Seal every cut end immediately.
5. Set each inboard joist segment (25 total) into its LUS210Z hanger at the ledger and its LUS210Z hanger on the mid-girder house-side face. Use a speed square to confirm each joist is perpendicular to the girder before nailing. Nail with N10 or SDS ¼"×1½" screws per Simpson LUS210Z spec sheet. Do not substitute.
6. Set each outboard joist segment (25 total) into its LUS210Z hanger on the mid-girder far face and its LUS210Z hanger on the outer-rim girder inboard face. Same perpendicularity check. Same fastener spec.
7. Confirm the center joist row is at exactly **x=11'-6" framing centerline** — measured from x=0' (left end ledger face) on the ledger, mid-girder, and outer-rim girder faces. This is the joist that the center divider breaker will face-screw into. Precision here matters; if it is off, the finished breaker will be off-center and field plank counts will be unequal.
8. Do a crown check: all 25 joist rows must be installed crown-up. Joist tops must be level with the top of their respective hanger flanges and flush with the girder tops. Confirm with a straightedge or laser across the full 23-ft span.

**STOP. Do not apply joist tape yet. Call for framing inspection now.** The inspector must see all hangers, fastener patterns, lateral ties, and ledger connection on bare wood. Tape goes on only after inspection passes.

9. After framing inspection passes: apply Vycor Deck Protector or Trex Protect 2" tape to the top face of every joist segment, both girder top runs, and the full ledger top. Apply the full length of each member; butt-joint and overlap tape at intersections. Press firmly; tape must lay fully flat. Total tape coverage: ~515 lf (50 joist segments × ~8 lf each = ~400 lf; both girder runs ~92 lf; ledger top ~23 lf).

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **Joists run PERPENDICULAR to the house — into the yard (y-direction).** In v15, joists ran parallel to the house (x-direction). v17 is the reverse. If a framer starts laying out joists parallel to the house, stop the work. Girders go wide (x-direction); joists go deep (y-direction).
- 🟥 **Every joist row has TWO segments split at the mid-girder.** There is no continuous joist running from the ledger all the way to the outer-rim girder. Each row is two shorter pieces, each about 7'-11¼". Do not install full-depth joists that bypass the mid-girder.
- 🟥 **12" o.c. joist spacing, not 16".** 25 rows across 23'-0". Confirm layout marks before installing a single hanger.
- 🟥 **LUS210Z fastener spec is specific.** N10 (10d×1½") or SDS ¼"×1½" per Simpson spec sheet. Do not substitute 16d commons. Read the spec sheet before loading your gun.
- 🟥 **Joist tape goes on AFTER framing inspection passes, BEFORE first decking plank.** Do not tape before inspection — inspector needs bare wood to see hanger nail patterns. Do not skip taping — MoistureShield warranty and best practice require it.
- 🟥 **Center joist at x=11'-6" framing is off-grid.** It falls between the regular 11'-0" and 12'-0" rows. It must be precisely at 11'-6" — not 11'-0", not 11'-3", not eyeballed. Measure from x=0' with a tape.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] All 25 joist rows installed; joist spacing confirmed at 12" o.c. across full run
- [ ] Center joist confirmed at exactly x=11'-6" framing by tape from x=0' at ledger, mid-girder, and outer-rim girder
- [ ] All joist tops are crowned-up and level with each other — within 1/8" across the full deck
- [ ] All LUS210Z hangers fastened with specified nail/screw (N10 or SDS ¼"×1½") — no substitutions
- [ ] **Framing inspection PASSED — inspector signed off on bare framing before tape was applied**
- [ ] Vycor / Trex Protect joist tape applied to all joist segment tops, both girder top runs, and ledger top — fully pressed down, no lifting edges

### Inspection / sign-off note

The framing / pre-decking inspection is the most critical inspection in the build sequence. West Hartford inspector typically checks: ledger connection (HU210-2 hanger + bolt pattern), through-bolt back-blocking, hanger nail/screw spec compliance, joist spacing, DTT2Z lateral tie placement, and pier saddle condition. Have all Simpson spec sheets on-site. Schedule 2–3 business days in advance. Do not apply joist tape until this inspection is signed off.

## Section 6 — Blocking Installation (Phase 6)

### What you're doing

Installing two rows of solid blocking between joist segments for lateral stiffness. Blocking rows run x-direction (perpendicular to joists), one row per joist bay at each bay's mid-span.

### Materials needed

- 2×10 PT UC4B blocking cuts — ~46 pieces at ~10¼" each (v17 BOM Section 2d). Cut from 8-ft drop-offs from joist material (each 8-ft board yields one ~7'-11¼" joist segment + a ~1" offcut — not enough for blocking; use dedicated 8-ft boards or order 4 extra).
- Construction adhesive — PL Premium or equivalent
- Toenail fasteners or Simpson A35 angle clips

### Tools needed

Circular saw, drill-driver, hammer, tape measure.

### Steps

1. Mark two blocking row positions on all joist top faces:
   - **Row 1 at y=4'-0"** — mid-span of the inboard joist bay (between ledger and mid-girder)
   - **Row 2 at y=12'-0"** — mid-span of the outboard joist bay (between mid-girder and outer-rim girder)
2. Cut blocking pieces from 2×10 PT stock to fit the joist bay width at 12" o.c. spacing: approximately 10¼" between joist faces. Measure each bay independently — bay widths may vary slightly along the run. Seal every cut end.
3. Apply a bead of PL Premium construction adhesive to the blocking contact faces before installing (belt-and-suspenders at tight butt joints). Install blocking snug between joist faces. Toenail through joist faces or use Simpson A35 angle clips at each end.
4. Stagger blocking slightly (offset alternate pieces by 1") to allow end-nailing through joist faces without splitting.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **Blocking runs x-direction (perpendicular to joists).** Joists run y-direction; blocking bridging between them runs x-direction, parallel to the girders. Do not run blocking parallel to joists.
- 🟥 **Blocking rows are at y=4'-0" and y=12'-0", not at x-positions.** These are positions along the deck depth (y-direction), at the mid-span of each of the two 8-ft joist bays.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] Blocking Row 1 installed at y=4'-0" — one block per joist bay, full 23'-0" run (23 joist bays)
- [ ] Blocking Row 2 installed at y=12'-0" — one block per joist bay, full 23'-0" run
- [ ] All blocking cut ends sealed
- [ ] All blocks fastened — toenailed or A35 clips at each end

## Section 8 — Decking — First Half (Phase 8)

### What you're doing

Laying one half of the field decking with Tiger Claw TC-G hidden clips. All planks run x-direction (parallel to the house). Each plank is 11'-9" cut from 12-ft Vision stock. The plank end on the inboard end butts the house-side PF inboard edge; the plank end on the far end butts the center joist face at x=11'-6" framing position (which will be covered by the center divider breaker in Phase 9). Work from the house-side PF inboard edge toward the center. Alternatively, start from the side PF inboard edge and work toward the center — either direction is acceptable; choose based on site access and staging.

**Owner's choice:** Left half first (x=0' to x=11'-9") or right half first (x=11'-9" to x=23'-0"). Document which half you start with in the build log for the center divider installation.

### Materials needed

- MoistureShield Vision 12-ft field planks — first half: 32 planks cut to 11'-9" each (v17 BOM Section 3a)
- Tiger Claw TC-G hidden fastener clips — first half: ~350 clips (v17 BOM Section 5d)
- Stainless screws for TC-G clips × ~350 (v17 BOM Section 5d)
- ⅛" spacer gauges — paint-stir sticks or Tiger Claw spacers (v17 BOM Section 10)
- End-cut sealant per MoistureShield installation guide
- #2 Robertson (square) driver bits × 5 minimum

### Tools needed

Circular saw with 40-tooth carbide blade (composite), drill-driver, chalk lines, tape measure, knee pads.

### Steps

1. Confirm joist tape is fully applied and framing inspection has passed before the first plank is set. (This should already be complete from Section 5, Step 9.)
2. Snap a chalk line at x=11'-6" framing across all joist tops. This is the target edge for all plank ends on the center side.
3. Set the first field plank row at the house-side PF chalk line (2.4" inboard of house-side rim). Measure and mark the plank cut length: **11'-9"** from the house-side PF inboard chalk line to the center joist face chalk line at x=11'-6". Set the plank in position — do not fasten yet. Confirm fit before batch-cutting the rest.
4. Batch-cut all planks for the first half to 11'-9" from 12-ft stock. Apply end-cut sealant to both cut ends of each plank per MoistureShield's installation guide. Let sealant tack before installing.
5. Fasten the first plank row using Cortex face-screws at the house-side PF position (the PF board itself will cover this row's house-side end — face-screw is the correct detail here; hidden clips cannot be used at the dead end against the PF). Pre-drill all Cortex face-screw holes. Drive face-screws and set color-matched plugs flush with the Cortex driver tool.
6. Install Tiger Claw TC-G clips at each joist crossing for all subsequent plank rows. One clip per joist per plank gap side. Drive TC-G clips with the #2 Robertson bit. Use ⅛" spacer gauges to maintain consistent gaps. Keep extra #2 bits — composite decking chews through bits on a long run.
7. Continue laying plank rows across the first half, working from the house-side PF inward toward the center. Every plank: 11'-9" cut length, end-cut sealed, consistent ⅛" gaps, TC-G clips at every joist crossing.
8. Stop when the last plank row's far end (toward the center of the deck) aligns with the chalk line at x=11'-6" framing. The plank ends should be square and flush at that line. **Do not cross the center joist with field planks.** The center divider breaker in Phase 9 will sit on top of and over these ends.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **Field planks run PARALLEL to the house (x-direction).** This is correct and matches v17's rotated geometry. Planks bridge across joist rows, each plank spanning from house-side PF to center breaker (or from center breaker to far-side PF on the second half).
- 🟥 **Each plank is 11'-9", not 15'-7" (v15) and not 16'-ft (do not use full boards).** The cut length is 11'-9" from a 12-ft board. The 3" offcut is scrap. Keep the saw station clean.
- 🟥 **Plank end-cut sealant is required by MoistureShield.** Apply immediately after cutting, before installation. Do not skip.
- 🟥 **Do not cross the center joist x=11'-6" with field planks.** The center divider covers those ends. If planks run past the line, the breaker will not seat correctly and the center gap will be non-uniform.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] First half of field decking installed — 32 plank rows
- [ ] All planks are 11'-9" cut length
- [ ] End-cut sealant applied to all cut ends
- [ ] Deck surface is square — no visible skew
- [ ] Plank gaps consistent at ⅛" per MoistureShield spec
- [ ] **Plank ends along the center joist line are straight within 1/16" over the full run** (snap a chalk line and check against it)
- [ ] All TC-G clips properly driven at every joist crossing
- [ ] No plank cupping or proud edges visible

## Section 9 — Center Divider / Breaker Board Installation (Phase 9)

### What you're doing

Installing the MoistureShield Vision center divider breaker board centered on x=11'-9" finish position (which is x=11'-6" framing centerline). The breaker is face-screwed into the center joist below it. It covers the plank ends from Phase 8 and will cover the plank ends from Phase 10 (second half). This is a v17-specific design feature — it is the visible seam between the two halves of the deck and functions as a clean architectural detail.

### Materials needed

- MoistureShield Vision center divider breaker board, 16'-0" continuous (v17 BOM Section 3b)
- Cortex face-screws + color-matched Vision plugs — 16 screws + 16 plugs for 12" o.c. along 16'-0" (included in v17 BOM Section 5d Cortex count)
- Cortex driver bit (confirm in Cortex box)
- Drill/driver

### Tools needed

Circular saw with 40-tooth carbide blade, drill-driver, tape measure, chalk line, clamps for positioning.

### Steps

1. Confirm the center joist (x=11'-6" framing) is clean, joist tape is intact on top, and the first half of decking (Phase 8) is fully installed with plank ends flush at the center chalk line.
2. Measure the actual finished length of the run from house-side PF inboard face to far-side PF inboard face. This should be approximately 16'-0" (the full deck depth minus the two PF board widths). Confirm before cutting.
3. Cut the breaker board to the measured length. Apply end-cut sealant to both cut ends.
4. Position the breaker board centered on x=11'-9" finish position (measured from the left PF inboard edge: 11'-9" to the center of the breaker). With the 5.4" actual board width, the breaker face centerline should be at 11'-9" finish. Clamp in place; confirm position with a tape from both left and right PF inboard edges.
5. Pre-drill all Cortex face-screw holes at 12" o.c. down the 16'-0" run — 16 holes total. Pre-drilling through capped composite is not optional; the cap layer will delaminate if screws are driven without pilot holes.
6. Drive Cortex face-screws through the breaker board down into the center joist top at each hole. Snug-tight — do not overtighten.
7. Set all 16 color-matched Cortex plugs flush using the Cortex driver tool. Plugs must be flush with the breaker surface; proud plugs will catch feet and are a trip hazard.
8. Verify the breaker is straight along its full 16'-0" length. Sight down the board from the house-side end; check for bow. Any bow greater than ¼" over 16 ft is a defect — address before the second half of decking is installed.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **The breaker board must be continuous — no midspan splice.** If your 16-ft or 20-ft board was cut short, order a replacement. A spliced breaker at mid-run will be visible and will not perform the same function.
- 🟥 **The breaker must cover all field plank ends from Phase 8 completely.** If any Phase 8 plank end is past the center chalk line and peeking out from under the breaker, trim that plank end back before installing the breaker.
- 🟥 **The breaker is a face-fastened board — plugs are visible.** Get the Cortex colorway right. This is the centerpiece of the deck's visual field and the most visible face-fastened element.

### Stop-and-verify gate ✓

- [ ] Breaker board is straight, continuous, full 16'-0" length
- [ ] Breaker is centered on x=11'-9" finish position (verified by tape from both left and right PF inboard edges)
- [ ] Breaker is face-screwed at every joist crossing (16 screws at 12" o.c.) — all screws seated fully
- [ ] All 16 Cortex plugs flush with breaker surface
- [ ] Breaker covers all Phase 8 plank ends with no exposed cut ends visible from above

### Tools needed

Circular saw, drill-driver, impact wrench, 4-ft level, string line, tape measure, shovel, hand tamper.

### Steps

1. Prepare the gravel pad outside the patio: mark an 18"-wide strip from x=11'-9" to x=23'-0", running from the patio edge (y=16'-6") outward. Lay woven landscape fabric, then place and compact a 4" bed of #57 crushed stone. Level the pad across the full 11'-9" run.
2. Set five precast deck blocks on the gravel pad at x = 23'-0", 20'-2", 17'-4", 14'-6", and 11'-9". Level each block; check level across all five with a string line or 4-ft level. These blocks must be level with each other so the ribbon is level.
3. Cut the step ribbon inner ply (2×6 PT, 11'-9") from 12-ft stock. Seal cut ends. Set it on the deck blocks temporarily to confirm fit. Then mount it permanently to the outer face of the outer-rim girder at the correct height so the step tread top lands at **6" AG**. Lag-bolt with ½" × 3" HDG lags at ~12" o.c. in two rows. **Confirm the connection method (lags vs. through-bolts) with the West Hartford inspector before driving** — IRC interpretation varies.
4. Install the outer ribbon ply (same 2×6 PT, 11'-9"). Through-bolt or structural-screw the two plies together at ~2 screws per lineal foot (20 screws total).
5. Install the two MoistureShield Vision tread planks (5.4" × 11'-9") on top of the doubled ribbon. Two planks side-by-side = 10.9" tread depth. Fasten with Cortex face-screws + plugs; pre-drill all holes. Plugs flush.
6. Confirm the 6" AG step-top elevation with a level and tape.

### Don't Forget — specific to this deck

- 🟥 **The five precast blocks are NOT frost-depth protected.** They will heave ¼–½" each winter and resettle each spring. This is the accepted design. Do not grout the blocks in place. Do not use hydraulic cement to anchor them. Check level each spring; tamp gravel and re-level as needed.
- 🟥 **Blocks sit OUTSIDE the patio footprint (y > 16'-6").** They sit on native soil over gravel, not on concrete. Make sure your gravel pad starts at the patio edge (y=16'-6"), not inside it.
- 🟥 **Confirm ribbon-to-rim connection method with the inspector before driving fasteners.** Do not assume lags are acceptable until confirmed.
- 🟥 **The 3" exposed patio strip is visible and intentional.** PF outer face is at y=16'-3"; patio edge is at y=16'-6". The 3" strip of bare concrete along the outer far edge is not a framing error. The right half (x=11'-9" to x=23'-0") is partially screened by this step assembly. The left half is open concrete — a purposeful narrow reveal.

## Appendix B — "This Deck Specifically" Cheat Sheet (v17)

Print this page. Read it on Day 1 before picking up a tool. Everything on this list is something a skilled framer with standard attached-deck experience would get wrong on this deck if they didn't know the specific plan.

**1. Girders run PARALLEL to the house (x-direction), not into the yard.**
In v15, girders ran from the house into the yard (y-direction). In v17 it is reversed. Two girder lines run x-direction (parallel to the house), like two rows of beams. Joists then bridge those beams y-direction (into the yard). If a framer starts setting girders running into the yard, stop the work.

**2. Joists run PERPENDICULAR to the house (y-direction), not parallel.**
In v15, joists ran parallel to the house. In v17, joists run into the yard. Each row is in two segments split at the mid-girder. If a framer starts laying out joists parallel to the house, stop the work.

**3. Field decking runs PARALLEL to the house (x-direction), not into the yard.**
In v15, decking ran perpendicular to the house. In v17 it runs parallel. Each plank is 11'-9" (not 15'-7"). If a framer starts running planks into the yard, stop the work.

**4. 8 piers in two rows of 4 — NOT 6 piers in two rows of 3.**
Mid-girder line: x=0', 7'-8", 15'-4", 23'-0" at y=8'-0". Outer-rim line: same x-positions at y=16'-0". Pier spacing is 7'-8" o.c. along each line. Count the marks before punching.

**5. Doubled 2×10 girder — NOT tripled.**
v15 used tripled 2×10. v17 uses doubled (2 × 1.5" = 3.0" wide) because the Pylex 10555 saddle is 3.5" wide — a tripled girder cannot seat properly. Do not use tripled-ply girders from v15 stock.

**6. Each joist row has TWO segments split at the mid-girder.**
No continuous joists from house to outer edge. Inboard segment: ledger to mid-girder. Outboard segment: mid-girder to outer-rim girder. 100 LUS210Z hangers total (not 75 as in v15).

**7. Center joist is at x=11'-6" framing — off the 12" grid.**
It falls between the regular rows at x=11' and x=12'. It is not one of those regular rows. It is an extra row at an exact intermediate position. Verify with a tape from x=0' before installing its hangers.

**8. Center divider breaker board is a v17-specific finish element.**
It sits at x=11'-9" finish (x=11'-6" framing), face-screwed into the center joist. It covers field plank ends from both halves and is the visible centerline of the deck. It must be continuous, straight, and color-match Cortex plugs. There is no equivalent in v15.

**9. HU210-2 hanger at ledger — NOT HU210-3.**
v15 used HU210-3 (3-ply girder). v17 uses HU210-2 (2-ply). Only TWO hanger locations at the ledger (x=0' and x=23'-0'), not three. The outer-rim girder does not connect to the ledger at all.

**10. Joist tape after framing inspection, before first plank.**
Do not tape before the inspector signs off. Do not set a single plank before taping. Both of these are absolute.

**11. 12" o.c. joist spacing — not 16".**
25 rows across 23'-0". 50 total segments. 100 hangers. This spec was chosen for stiffness under composite decking. Do not widen the spacing.

**12. MoistureShield Vision field planks are 12-ft boards cut to 11'-9".**
Not 16-ft. Not 20-ft (though 20-ft is the backup if 12-ft is unavailable). Each 12-ft board yields one 11'-9" field plank with a 3" waste offcut.

**13. Composite decking gaps are not optional.**
MoistureShield Vision requires ⅛" gaps between planks per their installation guide. This applies between field planks AND at the gap between field planks and the center divider breaker. Composite expands in summer heat. Butted planks buckle.

**14. CT 811 utility locate is mandatory before pier punching.**
Even through an existing patio slab. Mandatory. Failure to call is a legal and liability exposure.

## Appendix D — Owner's Notes / Troubleshooting

**If a pier stalls mid-drive and won't advance with normal rotation force:**
Stop immediately. Do not use a sledgehammer. Contact Pylex technical support. The helix may have hit a buried utility, a dense cobble layer, or the saddle weld is at risk. Reassess pier location if necessary. This is far easier to address before the girders are set.

**If a girder doesn't seat cleanly into a Pylex 10555 saddle:**
Check that your two ¼" shims (one per side) are in place inside the saddle and haven't shifted. If the girder still won't seat, check that the saddle top is at the correct elevation — it may need to be backed off slightly. Do not force the girder down into a misaligned saddle. Shim, adjust elevation, re-seat.

**If the framing inspection is scheduled and joist tape is already on:**
The tape must be removed from all areas the inspector needs to see (hanger nail patterns, especially). Peel it back as needed; re-tape after sign-off. Inform the inspector this happened and offer to show any specific hanger — proactive disclosure is better than a failed inspection.

**If field plank ends won't align at the center chalk line (Phase 8):**
Check that the center joist is truly at x=11'-6" framing — re-measure from x=0'. If it is in the right place but plank ends are varying, measure each plank individually rather than batch-cutting. Some boards from the same bundle can vary by ⅛" in actual length. Cut to a consistent chalk line, not to a consistent nominal cut.

**If a Cortex plug won't seat flush:**
Check that the pilot hole is deep enough (the Cortex screw must be set below the composite surface before the plug seats). The Cortex driver tool has a depth stop — confirm it is set correctly for the Vision board thickness (1.0"). If a plug is proud after proper seating, the pilot hole may be undersized — re-drill with the correct Cortex bit.

**If the far-edge step blocks heave more than ½" in spring:**
Check that the gravel pad is adequately drained and not retaining water that freezes under the blocks. The 4" gravel bed and landscape fabric are designed to allow drainage. If the area pools water, extend the gravel pad laterally to improve drainage. Do not anchor the blocks — heave and resettlement are expected and accepted.

**If miter corners at PF outside corners don't close cleanly:**
Composite PF boards can cup or bow slightly in temperature extremes. Re-cut the miters on-site at final assembly temperature. A small gap at the back of the miter (shadow side, not sun side) is acceptable cosmetically; a gap at the exposed face is not. Clamp and re-cut as needed.

**If any board shows cupping after installation:**
MoistureShield Vision has a capped top surface and an uncapped bottom. Cupping can happen if the bottom is significantly wetter than the top (e.g., boards stored face-down on wet ground). Ensure all boards are stored elevated, covered, and with the cap side up. A cupped board that has been installed should be removed and the board evaluated. Minor cupping that resolves within 2–3 days of installation is typically a storage condition and not a defect.
