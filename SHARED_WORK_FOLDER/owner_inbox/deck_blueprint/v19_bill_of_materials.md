## Scope

23'-6" × 16'-3" attached deck on existing concrete patio, West Hartford CT. Maximum 12" above finished grade. Attached to wood-frame house via 2×10 PT ledger on the 23'-6" long (house-parallel) edge — **note: ledger is 20'-0" long; see Owner's Note A for the supplemental-support requirement on the remaining ~3'-0" of the house-side edge.** Two doubled 2×10 PT girder lines parallel to house, each on 5 Pylex 50 screw piers — mid-girder at y=8'-0", outer-rim girder at y=16'-0". Face-hung 2×10 PT joists at 12" o.c., perpendicular to house, each spanning in two segments (ledger to mid-girder; mid-girder to outer-rim girder). Center joist at x=11'-6" framing centerline supports center divider breaker board. MoistureShield Vision composite decking parallel to house in two 11'-9" halves with center divider breaker, 4-sided picture frame (all four sides continuous), composite fascia on 3 exposed sides. All hardware ACQ-compatible (hot-dip galvanized or stainless).

### 2b. Girders (Doubled 2×10 PT)

Girders are **two lines running parallel to the house (x-direction)**, each a doubled 2×10 PT UC4B spanning 23'-0" between pier inboards.

**The fundamental rule: butt joints must land directly over a pier center.** A joint that falls between piers is a mid-span splice — never permitted in a two-ply built-up girder. With piers at x=0', 5'-9", 11'-6", 17'-3", and 23'-0", the splice recipe below is derived from the actual pier locations.

**Splice scheme (per girder line, 2 plies):**

| Ply | Pieces | Cut lengths | Joint locations | Source stock |
|-----|--------|-------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Ply 1 | 2 pieces | 11'-6" + 11'-6" | Butt joint over center pier at x=11'-6" | 2 × 12-ft 2×10 PT (6" waste each; drops usable for blocking) |

## 7. Owner's Notes (Hollis)

**A. 20-ft ledger — supplemental support required for the remaining ~3'-0" of the house-side edge.**
Thomas, this is the note I want you to see before anything gets ordered or permitted. The deck framing is 23'-0" wide, and the ledger is 20'-0" long. That leaves approximately 3'-0" of house-side edge — the last bay, roughly x=20'-0" to x=23'-0" — without a ledger to carry joist load back to the house. This is not a small detail. Before the permit is submitted, one of the following must be resolved:

- **(a) Supplemental short ledger segment:** A second ledger piece, roughly 3'-3" long, is installed and flashed to cover the remaining span to x=23'-0". Requires flashing, lag or through-bolt connections per DCA-6 requirements, and a lap detail or gap detail between the two ledger segments. This is the cleanest solution.
- **(b) Beam-and-post arrangement:** The last bay of joists at the house side is carried by a drop beam and post(s) rather than a ledger, moving that corner load to the patio/grade rather than the house framing. Requires confirming post bearing on a footing — the existing pier at x=23'-0" on the mid-girder line is at y=8'-0", not y=0'; a separate bearing point near y=0' at x=23'-0" would be needed.
- **(c) Designer/inspector confirmation:** The designer or West Hartford building official confirms that the joist framing in that zone is otherwise supported by the deck structure alone without a full-width ledger. This is the least likely path to acceptance without engineering documentation.

I am speccing the 20-ft board as instructed. Do not proceed to permit submission or framing without resolving which of (a), (b), or (c) applies. Flag this for the plans examiner — they will catch it anyway.

**B. DCA-6 Table 3A girder span — 29% margin.**
DCA-6 Table 3A, doubled 2×10 Southern Pine at 8-ft joist span column: maximum allowable girder span = 8'-2". Actual pier spacing in this design = 5'-9". That is 2'-5" under the DCA-6 limit — 29% margin. Print the DCA-6 table, circle the 8'-2" value, write "5'-9" actual" next to it, and put that sheet on top of your permit package. If the West Hartford plans examiner pushes back on the doubled girder or the parallel-to-house orientation, this number is your clean answer.

**C. Splice rule — do not let the contractor improvise this.**
Girder ply joints must land directly over a pier center. In this design, that means Ply 1 joint at x=11'-6" (center pier); Ply 2 joints at x=5'-9" and x=17'-3". These locations are non-negotiable. If a framer suggests splicing "wherever the board runs out," that is a structural deficiency and a permit failure. The splice diagram in Section 2b is the spec. Write it on the framing plan and post it at the job site.

**D. Saddle shim detail — 10 saddles, 40 shims.**
Each Pylex 10555 saddle gets a ¼" cedar or HDPE shim on each side before the doubled girder drops in — 40 shims total. Pre-cut and label them before pier installation day. A framer who skips the shims and drives the saddle bolts against a rattling doubled 2×10 will create a racking condition that shows up as a squeak or a bounce after the decking is down. Do it right on the way up.

**E. Saddle-to-girder carriage bolt connection.**
The girder-to-saddle connection uses ½" × 4" HDG carriage bolts through-bolted through the doubled girder and saddle plate — 2 bolts per saddle, 20 bolts total — rather than withdrawal-loaded lag bolts. This is a stronger connection: a full through-bolt transfers shear in bearing across the bolt cross-section, where a lag bolt in the girder bottom face relies on withdrawal resistance alone. Spec the bolts at 4" minimum length (3" of wood + saddle plate + washer + nut clearance); use 4½" if the Pylex 10555 saddle plate is thicker than ¼". Verify plate thickness before ordering. The saddle-to-shaft carriage bolts (attaching the saddle plate to the Pylex shaft itself) are a separate item — those stay as spec'd in Section 1.

**F. Geometric alignment — center pier, center joist, center breaker.**
The five piers on each girder line are evenly spaced at 5'-9". The center pier falls at x=11'-6". The center framing joist is at x=11'-6". The center breaker board sits at x=11'-9" finish (x=11'-6" framing). These three elements — center pier, center joist, center breaker — all converge on the same x-line. The pier is on the girder, not on the joist; structurally they're different systems. But the deck's natural midpoint is reinforced at every layer from the foundation up. It's a byproduct of the arithmetic, not a thing we engineered, but it's a clean result.

**G. Joist orientation — verify with the crew before framing.**
Joists run **perpendicular to the house** (into the yard). This is the reverse of the typical attached-deck framing instinct. Girders run parallel to the house; joists bridge between them into the yard. Verify with the crew foreman before framing begins.

**H. Pier layout verification before punching holes.**
Before punching any holes, lay out all 10 marks with marking paint, string line, and a chalk snap. Verify each mark falls within the patio footprint (23'-6" × 16'-6") and at the correct x and y positions. Do not start punching until all 10 are verified.

**I. Order PT lumber early.**
Kiln-dried-after-treatment (KDAT) is the right spec — drier, straighter, less post-install shrinkage. May need 1–2 weeks lead time at the yard. Call Richards Building Supply or Curtis Lumber (West Hartford area) early to confirm stock of KDAT SYP in the sizes needed. The girder stock is 12-ft boards — confirm 12-ft UC4B is available in quantity.

**J. West Hartford building permit.**
This deck is ≥ 200 sq ft attached to the house — a building permit is required. Bring to the permit office: the plan drawings, this BOM, the Pylex 50 + Pylex 10555 saddle load data sheets, the DCA-6 Table 3A girder span check (5'-9" actual vs. 8'-2" max = 29% margin), the Simpson LUS210Z and HU210-2 spec sheets, and the Simpson DTT2Z lateral tie spec sheet. The contractor pulls the permit — not the homeowner.

**K. No railing required by code.**
IRC R312 requires guardrail at decks ≥ 30" AG. This deck is ≤ 12" AG throughout. Confirm with the West Hartford building department at permit application.

**L. End-grain sealing is not optional.**
Every cut end of every PT board must be sealed with Copper Green or Anchorseal 2 immediately after cutting. Keep the can at the saw station.

**M. Cortex color — order after colorway is selected.**
Do not order Cortex plugs before the MoistureShield Vision colorway is finalized.

**N. Joist tape coverage.**
~515 lf total. Buy 11 × 50-lf rolls. Apply after framing inspection passes and before the first decking plank is set.
