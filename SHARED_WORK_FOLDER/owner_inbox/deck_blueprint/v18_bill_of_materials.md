# West Hartford Deck — v18 Bill of Materials

**Date:** 2026-05-10
**Prepared by:** Hollis, Home-Improvement Concierge / Owner's Advocate
**Change from v17:** Foundation upgraded from **4 piers per girder line to 5 piers per girder line** — 10 Pylex 50 piers total (was 8). Pier spacing along each girder line drops from 7'-8" to **5'-9"**, reducing the design girder span by 24% and increasing margin against the DCA-6 Table 3A limit from 6% to 29%. The new pier layout forces a revised girder splice recipe (the old 16-ft + 8-ft per ply scheme would create a forbidden mid-span joint between the 11'-6" and 17'-3" piers). v18 adopts a staggered three-piece splice: Ply 1 is two 11'-6" lengths butted at the center pier (x=11'-6"); Ply 2 is 5'-9" + 11'-6" + 5'-9" with joints over piers 2 and 4 (x=5'-9" and x=17'-3"). As a bonus, the center pier on each girder line lands at x=11'-6" — directly in line with the center framing joist and center breaker board. All other v17 decisions (rotated joists, doubled 2×10 girders, center joist at x=11'-6", center breaker at x=11'-9" finish, 72 × 12-ft Vision decking cut to 11'-9") are unchanged.

---

## Scope

23'-6" × 16'-3" attached deck on existing concrete patio, West Hartford CT. Maximum 12" above finished grade. Attached to wood-frame house via 2×10 PT ledger on the 23'-6" long (house-parallel) edge. Two doubled 2×10 PT girder lines parallel to house, each on **5 Pylex 50 screw piers** — mid-girder at y=8'-0", outer-rim girder at y=16'-0". Face-hung 2×10 PT joists at 12" o.c., perpendicular to house, each spanning in two segments (ledger to mid-girder; mid-girder to outer-rim girder). MoistureShield Vision composite decking parallel to house in two 11'-9" halves with center divider breaker, 4-sided picture frame, composite fascia on 3 exposed sides. Two egress points: left-edge single-step stair (42" wide, v12, unchanged) and far-edge wide step (11'-9" long, v13/v14, unchanged). All hardware ACQ-compatible (hot-dip galvanized or stainless).

### 2b. Girders (Doubled 2×10 PT) — PRIMARY CHANGE

Girders remain **two lines running parallel to the house (x-direction)**, each a doubled 2×10 PT UC4B spanning 23'-0" between pier inboards. The splice recipe changes completely from v17 because the new pier locations make the old 16-ft + 8-ft cut plan illegal.

**The fundamental rule: butt joints must land directly over a pier center. A joint that falls between piers is a mid-span splice — it is never permitted in a two-ply built-up girder. With piers at x=0', 5'-9", 11'-6", 17'-3", and 23'-0", a 16-ft board starting at x=0' ends at x=16'-0", which is 4'-6" past the pier at x=11'-6" and 1'-3" short of the pier at x=17'-3" — forbidden. The v18 splice recipe below solves this.**

**v18 splice scheme (per girder line, 2 plies):**

| Ply | Pieces | Cut lengths | Joint locations | Source stock |
|-----|--------|-------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Ply 1 | 2 pieces | 11'-6" + 11'-6" | Butt joint over center pier at x=11'-6" | 2 × 12-ft 2×10 PT (6" waste each; drops usable for blocking) |
| Ply 2 | 3 pieces | 5'-9" + 11'-6" + 5'-9" | Joints over piers at x=5'-9" and x=17'-3" | 1 × 12-ft 2×10 PT (center piece) + 2 × 8-ft 2×10 PT (end pieces, 2¼" waste each) |

> Ply 1 and Ply 2 joints are staggered to different piers — joints never occur at the same cross-section in both plies simultaneously. This is the mandatory stagger rule; do not deviate.

**Board count per girder line (1 girder, 2 plies):**
- Ply 1: 2 × 12-ft boards
- Ply 2: 1 × 12-ft board + 2 × 8-ft boards
- **Total per girder line: 3 × 12-ft + 2 × 8-ft**

**Board count for 2 girder lines (no spare):**
- 6 × 12-ft + 4 × 8-ft

**Order with 1 spare board each length:**
- **7 × 12-ft 2×10 PT UC4B + 5 × 8-ft 2×10 PT UC4B** for girders

> **Order: 7 × 12-ft 2×10 PT UC4B + 5 × 8-ft 2×10 PT UC4B for girders.**
>
> **Splice rule (mandatory):** Butt joints must land **directly over a pier center**, never midspan. Stagger plies so joints fall on different piers: Ply 1 joint at x=11'-6" (center pier); Ply 2 joints at x=5'-9" and x=17'-3" (piers 2 and 4). This stagger ensures the doubled girder is never both plies unsupported at the same cross-section. Do not allow both ply joints to land over the same pier, and do not allow any joint to land between piers.
>
> **Why the v17 splice recipe does not carry over:** v17 used 16-ft + 8-ft per ply, with joints over piers at x=7'-8" (Ply 2) and x=15'-4" (Ply 1). Those pier locations do not exist in v18. A 16-ft board from x=0' ends at x=16'-0", which is mid-span between the v18 piers at x=11'-6" and x=17'-3" — a prohibited location. Always re-derive splice cuts from the actual pier layout.
>
> **Structural note:** 5 piers create a 4-span continuous beam. The maximum bending moment in a continuous multi-span beam is lower per span than in a simply-supported configuration, and lower than v17's 3-span layout at the same total load. This is a structurally generous arrangement.
>
> **Saddle-fit rationale (unchanged):** A doubled 2×10 (2 × 1.5" actual = 3.0" total width) fits inside the Pylex 10555 saddle (3.5" interior) with ¼" of clearance per side — shimmed plumb with the ¼" cedar/HDPE shims specified in Section 1.

## Notes for the Homeowner / Contractor

**A. Order PT lumber early.** Kiln-dried-after-treatment (KDAT) is the right spec — drier, straighter, less post-install shrinkage. May need 1–2 weeks lead time at the yard. Call Richards Building Supply or Curtis Lumber (West Hartford area) early to confirm stock of KDAT SYP in the sizes needed. Note that v18 no longer requires 16-ft 2×10 PT stock for girders — 12-ft boards are the girder stock. Confirm 12-ft UC4B is available in quantity.

**B. v18 structural rationale — why 5 piers and why the splice recipe changed.** Adding a fifth pier per girder line drops the pier-to-pier spacing from 7'-8" to 5'-9" — a 25% reduction. Against DCA-6 Table 3A's 8'-2" limit for doubled 2×10 SP at 8-ft joist span, v18 has 29% margin vs. v17's 6%. More importantly, 5 piers create a 4-span continuous beam. A continuous beam distributes load more evenly than a series of simply-supported spans — maximum bending moment per span is lower at equal total load. This framing is structurally generous. The trade-off is 2 additional pier holes and 2 additional saddles; the lumber cost barely changes (12-ft boards replace 16-ft boards in the girder order).

**C. DCA-6 Table 3A girder span check — updated for v18.** Doubled 2×10 Southern Pine at 8-ft joist span: maximum allowable girder span = 8'-2". v18 pier spacing = 5'-9" — **29% under the limit**. Bring the DCA-6 reference to the West Hartford permit office. The plans examiner will want to see this check, especially because the girder orientation is non-standard. v18's wider margin makes this a simpler conversation than v17.

**D. IRC R507 joist span check — unchanged.** Per IRC R507 prescriptive spans, a 2×10 SYP at 12" o.c. supports joist spans well beyond 8 ft. The v18 joist segments are each ~7'-11¼" — well within allowable limits. No engineering letter should be required for this span.

**E. Saddle shim detail — 10 saddles in v18.** Each Pylex 10555 saddle gets two ¼" shims, one per side, installed before the doubled girder is lowered in. Shims must be: (a) rot-resistant — cedar or HDPE only, not OSB, not plywood; (b) sized to bear the full saddle side-wall face — minimum 1.5" × 3.5" plan area; (c) secured so they cannot walk out during saddle bolt tightening. The ½" HDG lag bolts through girder bottom into saddle (2 per saddle) lock everything in place. Do not skip the shims — an unshimmed doubled 2×10 in a 3.5" saddle can rack side-to-side under live load. v18 requires 40 shims total (was 16 in v17 — confirm your shim stock before install day).

**F. Girder splice rule — v18 version. This is not the same as v17.** v18 piers are at x=0', 5'-9", 11'-6", 17'-3", 23'-0". Every butt joint in every girder ply must land directly over one of those five pier centers. The v17 splice locations (x=7'-8" and x=15'-4") do not exist in v18. A contractor who copies the v17 splice plan will create mid-span joints — reject this immediately. The v18 stagger: Ply 1 joints at x=11'-6" (center pier); Ply 2 joints at x=5'-9" and x=17'-3".

**G. Joist orientation — unchanged from v17, still worth flagging.** In v18, joists run **perpendicular to the house** (into the yard). This is the reverse of the typical attached-deck framing instinct. Girders run parallel to the house; joists bridge between them into the yard. Verify with the crew foreman before framing begins. Nothing about this changed in v18 — the pier count increase is entirely in the x-direction (along the girder lines).

**H. Pier layout verification before punching.** v18 has 10 pier holes in 2 rows of 5. Before punching any holes, lay out all 10 marks with marking paint, string line, and a chalk snap. Verify each mark falls within the patio footprint (23'-6" × 16'-6") and at the correct x and y positions. The end piers at x=0' and x=23'-0" are the same as v17. The pier at x=11'-6" is new (center). The piers at x=5'-9" and x=17'-3" replace the v17 piers at x=7'-8" and x=15'-4" — do not confuse these.

**I. Center joist and center divider breaker — unchanged installation sequence.** Install the center framing joist (at x=11'-6" framing centerline) as part of the regular joist layout. It hangs in LUS210Z hangers at the ledger and both girder faces exactly like all other joists. After decking is installed on both halves up to the center bay, the MoistureShield Vision center divider breaker board is face-screwed down into the top of this center joist with Cortex screws + color-matched plugs at 12" o.c.

**J. Confirm 12-ft MoistureShield Vision stock before ordering.** The v18 field plank order of 72 × 12-ft Vision boards is unchanged. Confirm availability at Richards Building Supply before placing the order.

**K. Joist hanger count and nail/screw count — unchanged from v17.** 100 LUS210Z × 14 holes each = 1,400 fasteners. Buy 1,500 minimum. Same warning applies: do not substitute nail sizes.

**L. Joist tape coverage — unchanged from v17.** ~515 lf total. Buy 11 × 50-lf rolls. Apply after framing inspection passes and before the first decking plank is set.

**M. Cortex color — order after colorway is selected.** Do not order Cortex plugs before the MoistureShield Vision colorway is finalized.

**N. West Hartford building permit.** This deck is ≥ 200 sq ft attached to the house — a building permit is required. Bring to the permit office: the v18 plan drawings, this BOM, the Pylex 50 + Pylex 10555 saddle load data sheets, the DCA-6 Table 3A girder span check (5'-9" actual vs. 8'-2" max = 29% margin), the Simpson LUS210Z and HU210-2 spec sheets, and the Simpson DTT2Z lateral tie spec sheet. The West Hartford plans examiner will likely ask about the doubled (not tripled) girder — have the DCA-6 table ready. The 29% margin on girder span is a strong answer.

**O. No railing required by code.** IRC R312 requires guardrail at decks ≥ 30" AG. This deck is ≤ 12" AG throughout. Confirm with the West Hartford building department at permit application.

**P. Seasonal heave on far-edge step — unchanged.** The 5 precast blocks for the far-edge step are not frost-protected. Expect ¼–½" of seasonal lift and resettlement each year. Do not grout blocks in place. Check level each spring.

**Q. End-grain sealing is not optional.** Every cut end of every PT board must be sealed with Copper Green or Anchorseal 2 immediately after cutting. Keep the can at the saw station.

## Owner's Notes (Hollis)

**Permit-pack: DCA-6 Table 3A — v18 has a comfortable margin.**
DCA-6 Table 3A, doubled 2×10 Southern Pine at 8-ft joist span column: maximum allowable girder span = 8'-2". v18 pier spacing = 5'-9". That is 2'-5" under the DCA-6 limit — **29% margin**. Compare this to v17, which had 6" of margin (6%). Thomas, if the West Hartford plans examiner pushes back on the doubled girder or the parallel-to-house orientation, the v18 margin is a clean, defensible number. Print the DCA-6 table, circle the 8'-2" value, write "5'-9" actual" next to it, and put that sheet on top of your permit package.

**Splice rule — don't let the contractor wing this.**
Girder ply joints must land directly over a pier. In v18, that means Ply 1 joints at x=11'-6", Ply 2 joints at x=5'-9" and x=17'-3". These locations are non-negotiable. If a framer suggests splicing "wherever the board runs out," that is a structural deficiency and a permit failure. The splice diagram in Section 2b is the spec. Write it on the framing plan, laminate it if necessary, and post it at the job site.

**Saddle shim detail — 10 saddles, 40 shims.**
v18 adds 2 saddles over v17 — now 10 total. Each one gets a ¼" cedar or HDPE shim on each side before the doubled girder drops in. That is 40 shims total. Pre-cut and label them before pier installation day. A framer who skips the shims and drives the saddle bolts against a rattling doubled 2×10 will create a racking condition that shows up as a squeak or a bounce — and it's a pain to fix after the decking is down. Do it right on the way up.

**Geometric alignment — center pier, center joist, center breaker.**
Here is a clean coincidence that Thomas may appreciate. The five piers on each girder line are evenly spaced at 5'-9". The center pier falls at x=11'-6". The center framing joist runs at x=11'-6". The center breaker board sits at x=11'-9" finish (x=11'-6" framing). These three elements — center pier, center joist, center breaker — all converge on the same x-line. The pier is on the girder, not on the joist; structurally they're different systems. But visually and geometrically, the deck's natural midpoint is reinforced at every layer from the foundation up. It's a byproduct of the arithmetic, not a thing we engineered, but I'll take a clean coincidence when I see one.
