# Three 3D Printing Services — Quick-Reference Brief

*Prepared by Finn, 3D Print & Slicer Specialist | 2026-05-13*
*All three services verified active as of this date. Prices are estimates — always get an instant quote for your specific geometry.*

---

## 1. Craftcloud — Best All-Rounder / Aggregator

**What it's for:** Instant price comparison across 140+ vetted global manufacturers; the widest process and material menu in one upload.

**Processes & materials that matter:**
FDM, SLA, SLS, MJF, DMLS/SLM (metal), CNC machining. 31 technologies total, 200+ materials including stainless steel, titanium, carbon-fiber nylon, TPU.

**Strengths:**
- Instant online quoting with real-time price comparison from multiple suppliers — you see who's cheapest for your file right now.
- No minimum order, no service fee.
- "Best Local Price" filter lets you restrict to US-based manufacturers, eliminating customs duties and import delays entirely — a significant advantage given current trade policy.

**Concerns:**
- Craftcloud is the intermediary, not the printer. Quality consistency depends entirely on which partner fulfills your order. If something goes wrong, you're navigating a three-way relationship (you → Craftcloud → manufacturer).
- International orders (when local options aren't available) will add customs/duties on top of quoted price — not included by default.
- Lead times on the quote are estimates; actual production is in the partner's hands.

**Typical price range:** FDM small part ~$5–$25; SLA resin ~$10–$50; MJF nylon ~$30–$100; metal varies widely.

**Typical turnaround:** 2–5 business days production + shipping. US-local partners can ship faster; international adds transit time.

**When to pick Craftcloud:** You want to compare prices across multiple processes and suppliers in one upload, you're not sure which technology fits your part best, or you want a quick one-off prototype without committing to a specific service.

## 2. JLC3DP — Prosumer-Quality Dedicated Service

**What it's for:** Industrial-grade SLA resin and MJF nylon at aggressively low prices, from a large-scale Chinese manufacturing facility with 1,000+ printers.

**Processes & materials that matter:**
SLA (photosensitive resins), MJF (PA12, PA11, PAC, 3301PA, and others — six MJF nylon options), SLS (nylon powder), FDM, SLM (metal: stainless steel 316L).

**Strengths:**
- Some of the lowest prices available for MJF nylon and SLA resin — genuine access to industrial processes at near-hobbyist cost.
- Large material selection within each process (six MJF nylon variants is exceptional).
- Instant online quoting; order tracking comparable to standard e-commerce.
- Parts quoted starting from $0.30; MJF small parts often $5–$20 before shipping.

**Concerns:**
- **Active US tariff exposure (as of March 2026):** JLC3DP is China-based. US customers currently pay a pre-collected tariff surcharge at checkout — approximately **40% on plastic 3D prints, 70% on metal prints** (Section 301 + additional tariffs). This is the single biggest gotcha: a $15 MJF part becomes ~$21 before shipping. Rates subject to change with trade policy. *Verify current rates before ordering: [jlc3dp.com/help/article/us-tariff-policy-faq](https://jlc3dp.com/help/article/us-tariff-policy-faq)*
- Standard shipping to the US adds $8–$15 and 10–15 days transit. Express is $25–$50+.
- Dimensional accuracy averages ±0.2–0.3mm — fine for most prototypes, may fall short for press-fit or precision-critical parts.

**Typical price range:** SLA small part ~$3–$20 base (add ~40% tariff + shipping for US); MJF nylon ~$5–$40 base (same caveat); metal SLM parts significantly higher.

**Typical turnaround:** 2 days production claimed; add 10–15 days standard shipping to US (2–5 days express).

**When to pick JLC3DP:** You want the absolute lowest price on an MJF nylon or SLA resin part and the tariff math still makes it a win, or you're outside the US where the tariff concern doesn't apply.

## 3. Xometry — Industrial / Specialty Pick

**What it's for:** End-use-grade parts in metal (DMLS, binder jetting), production-quality MJF/SLS, Carbon DLS, and PolyJet — all from a US-based marketplace with certified manufacturing partners.

**Processes & materials that matter:**
DMLS (aluminum AlSi10Mg, stainless steel, Inconel, maraging steel, titanium), Metal Binder Jetting, HP MJF (nylon), SLS, SLA, Carbon DLS, PolyJet, FDM. 60+ materials total.

**Strengths:**
- Best-in-class for metal 3D printing (DMLS/binder jetting): real alloys with real mechanical properties, not decorative metal-fill FDM.
- Instant quoting engine accepts most CAD formats; parts can ship same-day or next-day on expedited tier.
- Free shipping on all US orders — no surprise freight costs.
- ISO 9001, ISO 13485, AS9100D, and IATF 16949 certified partners — suitable for medical, aerospace, or automotive prototype documentation requirements.
- No minimum order quantity.

**Concerns:**
- Price floor is higher than JLC3DP or Craftcloud's cheapest partners — small simple FDM or SLA parts can feel expensive relative to alternatives.
- Quality from their marketplace network can be inconsistent on very small machined features; some user reports of dimensional issues on sub-1" parts (machining, not 3D printing specifically, but signals a network-quality variance problem).
- Quote prices can vary significantly between identical orders placed weeks apart — the dynamic pricing model is opaque.
- Metal DMLS costs are genuinely high ($150–$2,000+ range); budget accordingly.

**Typical price range:** FDM small part $10–$50; MJF nylon $30–$150; DMLS metal $150–$2,000+ depending on size and alloy. PolyJet/Carbon DLS can exceed $200 for small parts.

**Typical turnaround:** 1–3 business days standard on most 3D printed parts; 1-day expedite available. Economy tier adds a few days for cost reduction.

## Decision Shortcut

- **If you need a one-off prototype in any material and want to shop prices fast** → Craftcloud (use the "Best Local Price" filter to stay US-side and skip tariff math).
- **If you need the cheapest possible MJF nylon or SLA resin part and the tariff math still pencils out** → JLC3DP (run the numbers including the ~40% surcharge before committing).
- **If you need a metal part, a certified material, or a part that has to work in the real world without excuses** → Xometry.

---

## Universal Gotcha

**Wall thickness minimums will reject or ruin your file at every one of these services.** FDM typically requires ≥1.2mm walls (2× nozzle diameter); SLA resin ≥0.5–1.0mm; MJF nylon ≥0.8–1.0mm; DMLS metal ≥0.5–1.5mm depending on alloy. Files that look fine on your QIDI will be auto-rejected or auto-thickened (badly) by online quoting engines. Check wall thickness in your slicer or in Meshmixer/Netfabb before you upload. Relatedly: most services accept STEP or 3MF for best fidelity — STL loses parametric precision and can introduce mesh errors that cause quote failures or unexpected price spikes from auto-repair.
