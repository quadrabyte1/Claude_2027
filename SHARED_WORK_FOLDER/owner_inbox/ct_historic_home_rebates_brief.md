### Owner's Advocacy Brief — Prepared by Hollis
*As of 2026-05-11. Verify currency on all programs before relying — several have funding caps, pending launches, or have recently changed.*

---

## 1. Which Registry Are You On? (You Need to Know Before You Apply)

There are three distinct categories. They overlap but are not the same, and different programs require different designations.

| Registry | Who Lists It | What It Means for Programs |
|---|---|---|
| **National Register of Historic Places (NRHP)** | U.S. National Park Service (NPS) | Qualifies for both federal and state credits |
| **Connecticut State Register of Historic Places** | CT SHPO (under DECD) | Qualifies for CT state programs; does not trigger federal credit alone |
| **Local Historic District** | Your town's Historic District Commission (HDC) | May require HDC approval for changes; may or may not qualify for state credits depending on whether property is also on State/National Register |

**How to look it up:**
- **CT database (ConnCRIS):** The CT SHPO maintains an interactive GIS map of all listed properties. Start here: [portal.ct.gov — Historic Property Database](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/content/historic-preservation/03_technical_assistance_research/research/historic-property-database)
- **CT SHPO directly:** Call 860-500-2300 or visit [portal.ct.gov — State Register](https://portal.ct.gov/DECD/Content/Historic-Preservation/01_Programs_Services/Historic-Designations/State-Registry-of-Historic-Places)
- **NPS National Register database:** Search the NPGallery at [npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP) or the NPS searchable spreadsheet at [nps.gov — National Register Database](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/nationalregister/database-research.htm)
- **Local designation:** Call your town's building or planning department and ask whether your address falls within a designated local historic district.

A property can appear on all three, two, or only one. Know which before you file anything.

## 2. The Headline Program: CT Historic Homes Rehabilitation Tax Credit (CGS §10-416)

**This is the most valuable program available to an owner-occupied historic homeowner in Connecticut. Do not skip it.**

- **Credit:** 30% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures (QREs)
- **Maximum credit:** $30,000 per dwelling unit ($50,000 for nonprofit owners — not applicable here)
- **Minimum spend:** $15,000 in qualified rehabilitation expenses
- **Maximum project cost for credit purposes:** $100,000 in QREs per application
- **Who qualifies:**
  - Owner-occupied 1–4 unit residential properties
  - Property must be individually listed on the CT State Register or National Register of Historic Places, *or* be a contributing resource within a listed historic district
  - At least one unit must be owner's primary residence for **five years** following credit issuance
- **How it's claimed:** Against Connecticut state income tax. The credit is issued as a voucher.
- **Transferability:** *Verify with SHPO* — the program has historically allowed transfer in limited circumstances; confirm current rules with DECD/SHPO before structuring your project around this.
- **Application portal:** [ctofficeofthearts.smapply.org — HHRTC Applications](https://ctofficeofthearts.smapply.org/prog/part_1_and_2_historic_homes_rehabilitation_tax_credit_applications/)
- **Official program page:** [portal.ct.gov — Historic Homes Rehabilitation Tax Credit](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/content/historic-preservation/02_review_funding_opportunities/tax-credits/historic-homes-rehabilitation-tax-credit)
- **Funding cap:** $3 million per year in reservations. In 2025, SHPO issued 126 vouchers totaling ~$2.74M and received nearly 500 applications. The program is **oversubscribed** — not all qualified applications receive credits in a given cycle.
- **Pre-approval is mandatory.** You cannot start work and then apply. SHPO requires a minimum 30-day review period before any work begins. Filing late disqualifies you.

> **Hollis's note:** At $3M/year and ~500 applicants, the odds are real but not guaranteed. File early, file complete, and do not lift a hammer until Part 1 approval is in hand.

## 3. Federal Historic Preservation Tax Credit (IRS §47 / 26 USC §47)

**Plain language: this is almost certainly not available to you as an owner-occupant.**

- **Credit:** 20% of qualified rehabilitation expenditures
- **Who actually qualifies:** Income-producing (depreciable) properties only — rental buildings, commercial properties, bed-and-breakfasts operated as a business. The IRS explicitly excludes owner-occupied personal residences from this credit.
- **Why it matters to note:** Many homeowners hear "historic tax credit" and assume this applies. It does not apply if the property is your personal residence.
- **Exception:** If a legitimate portion of your property is income-producing (a bonafide rental unit, a commercial space, a lawfully operated B&B), that *portion* may qualify — but you'll need a tax professional to calculate the eligible share.
- **Source:** [portal.ct.gov — Federal Historic Preservation Tax Credit](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/content/historic-preservation/02_review_funding_opportunities/tax-credits/federal-historic-preservation-tax-credit) | [IRS §47 guidance](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/businesses/rehabilitation-tax-credit)

## 4. CT Historic Rehabilitation Tax Credit (CGS §10-416c) — Income-Producing Properties

Same caveat as Section 3.

- **Credit:** 25% of QREs (increases to **30%** if the project is in a federally designated Opportunity Zone or includes an affordable housing component — 20%+ rental units qualifying under CGS §8-39a)
- **Maximum credit:** Up to $4.5 million per project
- **Annual program cap:** $31.7 million in reservations per fiscal year
- **Who qualifies:** Buildings with 5+ residential units, mixed-use buildings, or nonresidential historic structures — *not* owner-occupied single-family or small multifamily primary residences
- **Can be combined** with the federal §47 credit if the project qualifies under both
- **Program page:** [portal.ct.gov — Historic Rehabilitation Tax Credit](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/content/historic-preservation/02_review_funding_opportunities/tax-credits/historic-rehabilitation-tax-credit)

## 5. CT Historic Restoration Fund (HRF) Grants — SHPO

**Homeowners are not eligible.**

HRF grants are available to 501(c)(3) nonprofits and municipalities only, for properties on the State or National Register. The program is currently in its 2026 application cycle (applications due late October 2026, awards voted on in January 2027).

If you are involved with a preservation nonprofit that owns or manages a historic property, this channel is worth pursuing. As a private homeowner, it is not.

- **Program page:** [portal.ct.gov — Historic Restoration Fund Grants](https://portal.ct.gov/decd/content/historic-preservation/02_review_funding_opportunities/grant-opportunities/historic-restoration-fund-grants)

## 6. Stackable Adjacent Programs

These do not require historic designation and can often be layered on top of the CT HHRTC — *provided* the work does not damage the historic character of the property (see Section 7).

### A. Energize CT / Eversource & UI Rebates (Active — Use These)

The workhorse program for older CT homes with poor envelopes. Administered through Eversource and United Illuminating.

- **Insulation:** Up to **$2.00/sq ft** for professionally installed attic insulation; maximum **$10,000 per home**. Covers attic floors, walls, rim joists, basement ceilings. Income-eligible households pay $0 (others pay 25%).
- **Air source heat pumps:** $250/ton (standard) up to $1,000/ton (energy optimization), up to **$10,000 combined incentives** for qualifying systems
- **Ground source / geothermal heat pumps:** Separate rebate track; verify current amounts at energizect.com
- **Key requirement:** Must use a participating contractor; equipment must meet efficiency standards
- **0.99% Smart-E Loan special rate** available for heat pump installations through June 30, 2026 (see Section 6C)
- **Source:** [energizect.com — Rebates & Incentives](https://www.energizect.com/rebates-and-incentives)

> **Historic-home compatibility note:** Insulation in an attic or basement generally does not affect historic character. Replacing windows or adding mechanical systems to the exterior *may* require SHPO review. Check before you act.

### B. Federal IRA Credits — STATUS CHANGE: Both §25C and §25D Terminated 12/31/2025

> **VERIFY CURRENCY — This is a major recent change.**

The "One Big Beautiful Bill" (Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) terminated both major IRA residential energy credits at the end of 2025:

- **§25C (Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit):** Expired 12/31/2025. No credit available for property placed in service after that date. This covered insulation, windows, heat pumps, and similar improvements — the credit that was worth up to $3,200/year. **It is gone for 2026 installations.**
- **§25D (Residential Clean Energy Credit):** Expired 12/31/2025. This was the 30% credit for solar panels, geothermal heat pumps, battery storage, and fuel cells. **It is gone for 2026 installations.**
- **Exception:** If you completed qualifying work by December 31, 2025 and have not yet filed your 2025 return, you may still claim either credit on that return. §25D credits (unlike §25C) had a carryforward provision under prior law — consult your tax professional.
- **Sources:** [IRS OBBB FAQ — §25C/§25D Changes](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/faqs-for-modification-of-sections-25c-25d-25e-30c-30d-45l-45w-and-179d-under-public-law-119-21-139-stat-72-july-4-2025-commonly-known-as-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-obbb) | [IRS — Residential Clean Energy Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/residential-clean-energy-credit)

### C. CT Green Bank — Smart-E Loan (Financing, Not a Rebate)

Not a grant or credit — this is **below-market financing** that reduces the monthly cost of energy upgrades.

- **Loan amounts:** Up to $50,000 (up to $75,000 with lender exception approval)
- **Eligible:** 1–4 unit owner-occupied homes and condos in CT with individual electric meters
- **Covered upgrades:** 90+ categories including insulation, windows, heat pumps, solar, floodproofing, plumbing
- **Current promotional rate:** 0.99% APR for heat pump installations through **June 30, 2026** (standard rate: 6.99%–7.99%)
- **Source:** [ctgreenbank.com — Smart-E Loans](https://www.ctgreenbank.com/home-solutions/smart-e-loans/)

> **Hollis's note:** The 0.99% rate expires June 30, 2026. If a heat pump is in your scope and you want to use Smart-E financing, start the process now — contractor scheduling and loan processing take time.

### D. CT HEAR / HOMES Rebate Programs — PENDING LAUNCH (Verify Before Relying)

Connecticut was allocated ~$99 million in IRA home energy rebate funds ($49.8M HOMES / $49.4M HEAR). As of the date of this brief, **these programs have not yet launched.**

- DEEP submitted its HEAR Implementation Blueprint to DOE in April 2025; launch targeted "early 2026" but is contingent on DOE approval.
- HOMES blueprints were being drafted for later 2025 submission; no launch date confirmed.
- **Do not count on these programs** for any project you are planning to start imminently. Check [portal.ct.gov/deep/energy/inflation-reduction-act-home-energy-rebate-programs](https://portal.ct.gov/deep/energy/inflation-reduction-act-home-energy-rebate-programs) before finalizing your project budget.
- Income eligibility: HEAR targets households under 150% of area median income; HOMES focuses primarily on low-income multifamily.

### E. Property Tax Abatement / Freeze — Town-by-Town (CGS §12-127a)

Connecticut law **authorizes** (but does not require) municipalities to abate property taxes on structures of historical or architectural merit. Whether your town offers this, and on what terms, varies entirely by local ordinance.

- **What the statute says:** A municipality may abate real property taxes if the current tax level materially threatens the continued existence of the structure (CGS §12-127a). If you later demolish or remodel in a way that destroys historic value, the abated taxes become immediately due.
- **What to do:** Contact your town assessor's office directly and ask whether the town has adopted an ordinance under CGS §12-127a. Some Hartford-area towns (Middletown has an active program) do; many do not.
- **Source:** [Justia — CGS §12-127a](https://law.justia.com/codes/connecticut/title-12/chapter-204/section-12-127a/)

## 7. Concerns — Read These Before Spending a Dollar

These are not suggestions. These are the patterns that cost homeowners the most money.

**Pre-approval is not optional — it is a hard stop.** Nearly every CT historic preservation tax credit program requires SHPO pre-approval before any physical work begins. Starting work without approval does not just reduce your credit — it eliminates it. The application portal, the 30-day review minimum, and the Part 1 / Part 2 filing sequence exist for a reason. File first. Build second.

**The Secretary of the Interior's Standards are non-negotiable.** The CT HHRTC requires that ALL work on the property — not just the work being claimed — meet the [Secretary of the Interior's Standards for Rehabilitation](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/historicpreservation/rehabilitation.htm). Wrong windows (vinyl replacement windows in a pre-war building), wrong siding (fiber cement over historic clapboards), wrong roofing (architectural shingles over a slate profile) can disqualify the entire project. The Standards apply project-wide, not line-item by line-item.

**"Historic experience" is not a credential.** Any contractor can claim it. Before hiring anyone for work on a certified historic structure, ask for specific addresses of prior historic rehabilitation projects and cross-check them against SHPO project records. A contractor without a documented portfolio of SHPO-approved work is a risk, regardless of how good their reviews are elsewhere.

**Verify historic consultant credentials.** Some consultants charge for nomination support or credit-application preparation without meaningful qualifications. Look for NCPE-affiliated professionals or members of the AIA Historic Resources Committee. CT SHPO maintains a list of consultants familiar with their review process — ask them for it.

**The §25C and §25D federal credits are gone for 2026.** Do not build a project budget assuming these credits are available. They expired December 31, 2025 and were not reinstated. If a contractor, consultant, or financial planner tells you otherwise, verify directly at IRS.gov before relying on it.

**The HHRTC program is oversubscribed.** With ~500 applications annually and only $3M in credits, qualified projects do not always get funded in the cycle they apply. Have a contingency plan for project financing that does not depend on the credit arriving on a specific schedule.

## 9. $15K Threshold — Clarification (2026-05-11)

*Thomas asked: "It looks like any improvement project has to be at the $15,000 level or higher, and we don't have any projects like that upcoming. Is that right?"*

### Direct answer: Yes — and no, you can't easily work around it.

**Yes, the $15,000 minimum is real and hard.** CGS §10-416 states that the owner is not eligible for a tax credit voucher unless qualified rehabilitation expenditures exceed $15,000. This is a floor, not a soft guideline. Below $15,000 in qualifying costs, you get zero credit — not a prorated credit, not a partial credit. Zero.

**But the more important question is: what counts toward that $15,000?** The answer is narrower than most homeowners expect, and that makes the threshold harder to clear than a headline project cost might suggest.

---

### 9A. What Is — and Is Not — a Qualified Rehabilitation Expenditure (QRE)

The following is based on SHPO program materials and the statute. This list matters because you can spend $25,000 on a project and still have only $9,000 in QREs if the wrong things are being replaced.

**Costs that DO count toward the $15,000 QRE minimum (hard construction costs on approved rehabilitation work):**
- Foundation repairs and structural work
- Exterior repairs: masonry repointing, siding, roofing (in-kind replacement of historic materials)
- Windows and doors (repair or historically appropriate replacement when original is beyond repair)
- Porches, chimneys
- Interior character-defining features: plaster, moldings, medallions, built-in historic millwork
- Mechanical systems: HVAC, plumbing, electrical (if part of approved rehab scope)

**Costs that do NOT count toward the $15,000 QRE minimum:**
- Architectural, design, legal, and financing fees
- Permits
- Landscaping, driveways, sidewalks, fencing
- New additions (except as required by code)
- New construction
- Appliances or fixtures that are not part of historic character
- The owner's own personal labor and tools
- Site improvements (unless required for disability access)
- Spray foam insulation (considered destructive to historic fabric — and ineligible)
- Replacement windows (e.g., vinyl double-hung replacements) — these are categorically ineligible because they destroy historic character
- Wholesale siding removal or covering
- Blinds, shades, lamps, carpets
- Work on outbuildings that do not contribute to the historic significance of the property
- **Any costs incurred BEFORE the Part 2 application is approved by SHPO** — these are disqualified regardless of whether the work itself would otherwise be eligible. This is a hard rule with no exceptions.

> **Hollis's note:** That last point is the one that burns people most often. If you fix your historic windows in October thinking you'll apply in November, those window costs don't count. Pre-approval is not optional — it is a condition of QRE eligibility, not just a procedural nicety.

---

### 9B. Can the $15,000 Be Reached by Aggregating or Phasing Work?

**The honest answer: phased projects are technically permitted under the program structure, but the rules create real practical constraints.**

Here is what is confirmed:
- The HHRTC application has a Part 1 (historic structure determination), Part 2 (proposed rehab plan approval), and Part 3 (completion certification with receipts).
- For phased projects, a tax credit voucher can be issued for the identifiable portion of the structure placed in service at each phase completion.
- Each phase must independently clear $15,000 in QREs. The program does not appear to allow you to accumulate $8,000 this year and $9,000 next year under a single application and reach $15,000 across phases — the threshold applies to each application/phase standing on its own.

**What this means practically:** If Thomas does $8K of QRE work in Year 1 and $9K in Year 2, that is two separate phases and neither clears the minimum. He would need to structure a single phase of work — approved in advance under a single Part 2 application — that results in more than $15,000 in qualifying construction costs. Bundling scope into one approved project is possible; splitting small work across years and counting it cumulatively across separate applications is not.

> **Important caveat on phasing rules:** The program instructions PDF (which CT SHPO has declined to make publicly accessible at this time) may contain additional guidance. **Thomas should call CT SHPO directly at 860-500-2300 and ask: "Can I bundle multiple small projects into a single Part 2 application and accumulate QREs under one project period?"** That question is answerable by a program officer in two minutes and worth the call before assuming anything.

**The federal model for reference:** The federal §47 historic tax credit (income-producing properties only) uses a 24-month "substantial rehabilitation test" period and allows phased accumulation within a single elected measurement period. The CT HHRTC is a distinct state program for homeowners and does not explicitly adopt the federal measurement period mechanics. Do not assume federal phasing rules apply here.

---

### 9C. What the Application Structure Tells You About Timing

- **Part 1** — Submit before work starts. Confirms the property's historic status for credit purposes.
- **Part 2** — Submit before work starts. Proposes the specific rehab scope. SHPO approves or conditions the plan. **No QREs accrue before this approval.** Minimum 30-day SHPO review.
- **Part 3** — Submit after work is complete. Includes receipts and photos. SHPO confirms the work was done as approved. Credit voucher is issued.

The structure makes clear this is a sequential, project-scoped credit — not an annual reporting credit you accumulate over time. Each application cycle is discrete.

#### (vi) National Trust / Preservation Nonprofit Small Grants

The National Trust for Historic Preservation's **National Fund for Sacred Spaces** and similar programs are institution-focused. For private homeowners, the National Trust's **Preservation Funds** are primarily available to nonprofits. The **Connecticut Trust for Historic Preservation** (Preservation Connecticut) focuses on advocacy, technical assistance, and tools — not direct homeowner grants. There is no confirmed small-grant program for individual historic homeowners in the Connecticut landscape at this time.

---

### 9E. Strategic Note — Hollis-Style

Thomas is right that the §10-416 credit isn't a tool for isolated small projects. It is structured as a discrete rehabilitation event, not a rolling annual credit. If his honest answer is "no significant projects coming," the truthful advice is: **set this credit aside for now and use Energize CT rebates for anything efficiency-related.**

The one scenario to keep in mind: historic homes in this age and condition class tend to accumulate deferred maintenance faster than owners expect. A roof that needs attention, masonry repointing on the chimney and foundation, window restoration on the primary facade, porch reconstruction — any one of those can clear $15K in QREs. The critical rule, which is worth repeating: **if Thomas ever decides to tackle a larger rehab, he must contact SHPO before spending a dollar, or those costs won't count.** Pre-approval is not optional.

The $15,000 minimum is also per qualifying-construction-cost, not per total project budget. A $20,000 project with $6,000 in architect fees, $3,000 in permits, and $11,000 in construction costs does not clear the threshold. The math needs to be done on the QRE column, not the total check written.

- **Hartford §12-127a ordinance status is unconfirmed.** The one call to the Assessor's Office (860-757-9640) could reveal a property tax benefit that applies regardless of project size or credit minimums.
